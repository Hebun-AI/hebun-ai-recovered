# 08 — Director Gate

*The final stage of the reasoning lifecycle — Director Approval.*

## What it means

The Director Gate is where reasoning **stops and the Director decides**. The recommendation, complete and explainable, is presented to the Director. The Director accepts, rejects, defers, or overrides it. This is the terminus of the cognitive lifecycle: reasoning has done its work, and authority passes — as it always was — to the Director.

**Execution is outside this phase.** What happens after approval — planning, orchestration, action — belongs to other layers and other phases. The cognitive model ends here, at the moment of the Director's decision.

## Why it exists

Because reasoning produces judgment, not action ([reasoning boundaries](../director-reasoning/03-reasoning-boundaries.md)), and the Director always owns final authority ([Director Authority](../director-reasoning/05-director-authority.md)). The gate is where these two Phase 7A principles become concrete: every chain of reasoning, no matter how sound, resolves to the Director's choice. The gate exists so that reasoning can be as capable as it likes while authority stays, absolutely, with the Director.

## Required inputs

- The **decision-ready recommendation** from the Recommendation stage — course, reasoning, alternatives, confidence, uncertainties, and commitment status.

## Expected outputs

- The **Director's decision** — accept, reject, defer, or override — captured explicitly.
- For anything **irreversible or committing**, the Director's **explicit approval** before it may proceed; without it, the action does not happen.
- The decision itself becomes **input to memory** (through a separate, gated recording act) so that future reasoning learns from it — approvals and refusals alike ([learning loop](../capabilities/personal-enterprise-mode/05-learning-loop.md)).

## Gate behavior

- **Explicit and per-decision.** Approval is given by the Director, for this recommendation, now. It is not inferred from the recommendation's confidence and does not generalize to future decisions.
- **Rejection is a valid outcome.** The Director declining is ordinary, not an error. Reasoning does not re-press a rejected recommendation as if the "no" were a fault.
- **Override is the Director's right.** When the Director chooses against reasoning's recommendation, the Director's choice governs. Reasoning may have stated its case honestly; it does not get the last word.

## Transition criteria

The lifecycle **ends** here. There is no next cognitive stage — the reasoning cycle is complete once the Director has decided. Any resulting action is handed to layers outside this phase; a new situation begins a new cycle at Observation.

The Director Gate is the fixed point of the whole model: **reasoning thinks all the way to a recommendation, and there it stops — the Director decides.**
