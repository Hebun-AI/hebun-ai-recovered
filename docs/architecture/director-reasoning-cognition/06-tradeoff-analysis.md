# 06 — Trade-off Analysis

*The stage after Option Generation.*

## What it means

Trade-off Analysis is where reasoning **weighs the options against one another**. Option Generation produced the field; this stage evaluates it — what each option gains and gives up, its opportunity and its risk, and its effect on the whole organization. This is the convergent stage: reasoning narrows the field toward a judgment.

Every option has costs and benefits; none is free. Trade-off Analysis makes those explicit and comparable, so that the choice ahead is an informed one.

## Why it exists

Because choosing well means seeing the costs, not just the benefits. This stage is where the Phase 7A [decision principles](../director-reasoning/04-decision-principles.md) do their work — prioritization, trade-off analysis, opportunity evaluation, risk evaluation, organizational impact, confidence. It exists so the recommendation that follows rests on a fair comparison, and so the Director can later see the *same tensions reasoning weighed*. Hiding a trade-off to make an option look clean would violate both truth and explainability ([first principles](../director-reasoning/02-first-principles.md)).

## Required inputs

- **Option set** from Option Generation, each with its committing status.
- **Goals** (to measure options against) and **constraints** (to weight soft limits and risk).
- The **relationship graph** for tracing organizational impact ([impact analysis](../relationship-graph/05-impact-analysis.md)) and **memory** for how comparable choices have gone before.

## Expected outputs

- **Weighed options** — for each option: what it gains, what it costs, its opportunity, its risk, and its organizational impact, laid out comparably.
- An emerging **ordering** — which options are stronger and why — with the reasoning explicit.
- **Confidence** in the comparison, and any material uncertainty that affects it, stated honestly.

## Transition criteria

Reasoning may move to Recommendation when:

- each option has been evaluated for gain, cost, opportunity, risk, and organizational impact;
- the trade-offs are explicit and comparable, none hidden;
- an honest ordering has emerged, with its confidence and uncertainties named.

If the options have not been fairly weighed — or a trade-off is being suppressed to favor one — reasoning stays here. A recommendation built on an unfair comparison is unsound.
