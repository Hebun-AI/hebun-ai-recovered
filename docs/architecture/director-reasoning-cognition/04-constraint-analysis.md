# 04 — Constraint Analysis

*The stage after Goal Identification.*

## What it means

Constraint Analysis is where reasoning establishes **what limits the space of acceptable action**. With the goal fixed, the engine identifies the boundaries any option must respect — what is forbidden, required, unavailable, or risky. Constraints define the edges of the field before options are generated inside it.

Constraints come from many sources: the organization's rules and governance, the workspace boundary, available resources, legal and compliance obligations, the Director's known limits, and — crucially — the **Director Gate** itself, which marks committing actions as requiring approval.

## Why it exists

Because options generated without constraints are fantasies — attractive on paper, impossible or unacceptable in reality. Constraint Analysis exists so that later stages explore only the space of *genuinely available* actions. It also front-loads the boundary that governs everything Hebun does: the line between what reasoning may prepare freely and what requires the Director's explicit approval ([reasoning boundaries](../director-reasoning/03-reasoning-boundaries.md), [Director Authority](../director-reasoning/05-director-authority.md)). Knowing the constraints early keeps reasoning honest and grounded.

## Required inputs

- **Identified goals** from Goal Identification.
- The applicable **constraints** knowable from the knowledge layers: governance and rules, workspace scope, resources, obligations, risk limits, learned Director preferences, and the committing-action boundary.

## Expected outputs

- **Constraint set** — an explicit account of what any option must respect: hard limits (forbidden, required, impossible) and soft limits (costly, risky, disfavored).
- A clear marking of which potential actions fall on the **gated side** — committing or irreversible actions that, even if pursued, will require explicit Director approval.

## Transition criteria

Reasoning may move to Option Generation when:

- the hard and soft constraints bounding the goal are explicit;
- the committing-action boundary is identified, so gated actions are flagged from the start;
- constraints are grounded in the knowledge layers, not assumed.

If the boundaries of the possible are still unclear, reasoning stays here. Generating options before knowing the constraints wastes the effort on the impossible.
