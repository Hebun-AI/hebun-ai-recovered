# Phase 34 — Policy & Continuous Security Governance

## Canonical Status

**STATUS: COMPLETE / PUBLISHED**

This document defines the canonical Enterprise constitutional architecture governing Security Policy and Continuous Security Governance within Program V — Enterprise Security.

Phase 34 governs Security Policy identity, authority basis, applicability, lifecycle, versioning, review, Approval, exception, compliance, consistency, retirement, evidence, conflict, continuity, and constitutional integrity. It does not define Governance implementation, policy engines, Policy-as-Code, Runtime enforcement, Security tooling, compliance frameworks, audit tooling, infrastructure, deployment, or product behavior.

Phase 34 is technology independent, Runtime independent, implementation independent, vendor independent, infrastructure independent, deployment independent, and product independent.

# 1. Purpose

Policy & Continuous Security Governance exists to preserve coherent, attributable, reviewable, and constitutionally valid Security direction across the Enterprise as obligations, risks, organizations, evidence, and operating conditions evolve.

Its mission is to:

- establish Security Policy as an authoritative but bounded constitutional object;
- preserve the origin, Scope, purpose, applicability, ownership, and Authority basis of every Security Policy;
- govern Policy lifecycle, versioning, review, Approval, exception, and retirement;
- distinguish Policy from law, standard, procedure, control, implementation, and Runtime enforcement;
- preserve Human constitutional supremacy and Director final Authority;
- evaluate Policy Compliance without converting evaluation into truth, Approval, enforcement, or execution;
- detect and expose Policy inconsistency, conflict, ambiguity, omission, and expiry;
- preserve Phase 30 Identity, Trust, Permission, and Authority;
- preserve Phase 31 Runtime and Execution Security ownership;
- preserve Phase 32 AI Security ownership;
- preserve Phase 33 Enterprise Security Operations ownership;
- keep Security Policy meaning stable across technologies, vendors, Runtimes, infrastructures, deployments, and products.

Phase 34 answers **how Enterprise Security Policy remains constitutionally valid, coherent, reviewable, and continuous**. It does not answer how policy is encoded, enforced, monitored, audited, or implemented.

## Purpose Rules

- **P34-DC-001 — Constitutional Policy:** Every Security Policy must remain subordinate to the Enterprise Constitution and Director Authority.
- **P34-DC-002 — Declared Policy Context:** Every Security Policy must declare identity, purpose, Scope, owner, Authority basis, applicability, lifecycle, version, and review boundary.
- **P34-DC-003 — Continuous Integrity:** Policy validity and consistency must remain reviewable throughout the applicable lifecycle.
- **P34-DC-004 — Non-implementation:** Phase 34 must not define policy engines, Policy-as-Code, enforcement mechanisms, Runtime behavior, tooling, infrastructure, deployment, or product behavior.

# 3. Core Principles

## Constitutional Supremacy

No Security Policy may contradict, replace, bypass, or silently amend the Enterprise Constitution.

## Explicit Authority

Every Security Policy must derive normative force from a recognized, attributable, Scope-bound, and revocable Authority basis.

## Human Constitutional Supremacy

Policy authorship, analysis, automation, urgency, or technical control cannot replace authorized Human judgment or Approval.

## Director Constitutional Authority

The Director retains final constitutional Authority over reserved Policy conflicts, amendments, exceptions, and strategic acceptance.

## Policy Transparency

Policy identity, owner, Authority basis, purpose, Scope, applicability, obligations, prohibitions, evidence, exceptions, versions, conflicts, and lifecycle must remain visible.

## Policy Traceability

Every Policy meaning and change must remain reconstructable from source Authority through authorship, review, Approval, version, applicability, exception, compliance evaluation, supersession, and retirement.

## Evidence-First Governance

Policy review, compliance, consistency, exception, and retirement claims must cite attributable evidence and expose uncertainty, contradiction, and limitation.

## Least Authority

Policy scope and normative effect must be no broader than the originating Authority permits.

## Separation of Policy and Enforcement

Policy declares governed Security direction. It does not itself enforce, execute, monitor, remediate, or control.

## Separation of Compliance and Approval

Compliance is an evidence-qualified evaluation. It is not Policy Approval, truth, Authority, permission, or execution eligibility.

## Version Integrity

Every Policy version preserves identity, lineage, effective meaning, Approval basis, applicability, and relationships to prior and later versions.

## Exception Explicitness

Policy Exception must remain explicit, minimal, attributable, justified, time-bound, reviewable, revocable, and unable to silently rewrite baseline Policy.

## Continuous Constitutional Governance

Policy integrity remains eligible for continuing review as evidence, Authority, obligations, risks, dependencies, and organizational conditions change.

## Technology and Environment Independence

Security Policy meaning remains stable across technologies, vendors, Runtimes, infrastructures, deployments, products, and organizational environments.

# 4. Canonical Definitions

> **Policy Lifecycle is the governed constitutional progression of a Security Policy through declaration, review, Approval, effective applicability, maintenance, amendment, supersession, retirement, and archival reference without defining workflow or Runtime state.**

> **Policy Governance is the constitutional responsibility for preserving Security Policy Authority basis, ownership, lifecycle, review, Approval, exception, compliance, consistency, traceability, and integrity without becoming implementation, enforcement, or final Director Authority.**

> **Policy Review is the attributable examination of a Security Policy’s Authority basis, purpose, Scope, applicability, evidence, obligations, prohibitions, consistency, exceptions, lifecycle, and constitutional compatibility by an eligible review context without becoming Approval or enforcement.**

> **Policy Approval is the published Human Approval act through which an authorized human accepts, rejects, defers, constrains, or revokes a bounded Security Policy proposal or committing Policy responsibility without transferring Director-reserved Authority or authorizing implementation by default.**

## Policy Exception

> **Policy Exception is an explicit, Authority-grounded, minimal, Scope-bound, purpose-bound, time-bound, attributable, justified, reviewable, and revocable qualification of one Security Policy requirement that preserves baseline Policy meaning, residual risk, accountability, evidence, and traceability.**

> **Policy Compliance is an attributable, evidence-qualified, time- and Scope-bound evaluation of whether a declared subject satisfies applicable Security Policy requirements without becoming truth, Approval, Authority, enforcement, certification, or execution eligibility.**

## Policy Consistency

> **Policy Consistency is the evidence-supported constitutional condition in which applicable Security Policies preserve compatible meanings, Authority boundaries, obligations, prohibitions, priorities, exceptions, and dependencies without unresolved contradiction.**

## Policy Version

> **Policy Version is an immutable, attributable, temporally bounded expression of one Security Policy identity that preserves exact meaning, Authority basis, Approval, applicability, lineage, and relationship to superseded or successor versions.**

## Policy Retirement

> **Policy Retirement is the governed constitutional act that ends a Security Policy version’s future applicability while preserving historical meaning, evidence, obligations, exceptions, lineage, accountability, and auditability.**

## Continuous Security Governance

> **Continuous Security Governance is the ongoing, evidence-based, implementation-independent constitutional responsibility for evaluating Security Policy validity, applicability, consistency, compliance, exception, review, and lifecycle needs without becoming Monitoring, Runtime control, enforcement, or execution.**

## Constitutional Policy Integrity

> **Constitutional Policy Integrity is the preserved alignment of every Security Policy with the Enterprise Constitution, originating Authority, approved meaning, Scope, applicability, version, lifecycle, evidence, exceptions, and accountability.**

> **Security Policy Authority is the recognized, explicit, Scope-bound, purpose-bound, attributable, reviewable, and revocable constitutional Authority basis from which a Security Policy derives normative force without creating new Authority through authorship, publication, access, or implementation.**

## Policy Applicability

> **Policy Applicability is the governed determination that a specific Security Policy version is relevant to a declared subject, Scope, organization, Tenant, purpose, time, classification, and responsibility context without establishing compliance or enforcement.**

# 6. Policy Lifecycle

Policy Lifecycle meanings are:

- **Declared:** a bounded Policy proposal has an attributable identity, owner, Authority basis, and purpose.
- **Under Review:** eligible reviewers are examining constitutional compatibility and evidence.
- **Approved:** an Approved Policy Version exists only after an authorized Human has produced an attributable accepting outcome through Policy Approval; rejection, deferral, constraint, or revocation does not establish the Approved state.
- **Effective:** an approved Policy Version is applicable within its declared effective boundary.
- **Maintained:** evidence and applicability remain under governed review without silent meaning change.
- **Amended:** a new proposed Policy Version carries attributable changes and lineage.
- **Superseded:** a successor version has replaced future applicability while preserving history.
- **Retired:** future applicability has ended through governed retirement.
- **Archived Reference:** historical meaning remains preserved for evidence, accountability, and traceability.

These are constitutional lifecycle meanings, not workflow stages, Runtime states, timestamps, automation, or implementation behavior.

## Lifecycle Rules

- **P34-DC-028 — Declared Identity:** A Policy must have stable identity before lifecycle qualification.
- **P34-DC-029 — Review Eligibility:** Review requires eligible reviewers, declared criteria, evidence, Scope, and Authority context.
- **P34-DC-030 — Approval Attribution:** Approval must identify the authorized Human, bounded act, Policy version, time, and Authority basis.
- **P34-DC-031 — Effective Boundary:** Effective applicability must preserve exact version, Scope, subject, time, and organization.
- **P34-DC-032 — No Silent Mutation:** Approved Policy meaning must not change without a new attributable version and review.
- **P34-DC-033 — Supersession Preservation:** Supersession must preserve historical versions, applicability, exceptions, evidence, and lineage.
- **P34-DC-034 — Retirement Non-erasure:** Retirement must end future applicability without erasing historical responsibility or evidence.

# 10. Policy Compliance, Consistency & Integrity

Policy Compliance evaluates a declared subject against an applicable Policy version and exact requirements. It preserves:

- subject identity and responsibility;
- Policy identity, version, and applicability;
- evaluation criteria and evidence;
- time, Scope, organization, Tenant, classification, and purpose;
- uncertainty, contradiction, limitation, exception, and missing evidence;
- evaluator attribution and accountable review boundary.

Policy Consistency evaluates compatibility among applicable Policies without silently selecting a winner or creating Authority.

Constitutional Policy Integrity evaluates whether Policy Authority, approved meaning, Scope, version, lifecycle, evidence, exception, and accountability remain aligned with the Enterprise Constitution.

Possible findings include compliant, non-compliant, indeterminate, conflicting, inapplicable, expired, or review required. Findings are evidence-qualified and do not enforce, approve, certify, or execute.

## Compliance and Integrity Rules

- **P34-DC-057 — Applicable Version:** Compliance requires an exact, currently applicable Policy version.
- **P34-DC-058 — Evidence-bound Finding:** Every finding must cite eligible evidence and expose uncertainty and missing evidence.
- **P34-DC-059 — Indeterminate Visibility:** Insufficient, contradictory, invalid, or expired evidence must produce an explicit indeterminate or review-required finding.
- **P34-DC-060 — Compliance Non-approval:** Compliance must not become Policy Approval, Human Approval, Authority, Permission, or certification.
- **P34-DC-061 — Compliance Non-enforcement:** Compliance findings must not enforce, remediate, control, or execute.
- **P34-DC-062 — Consistency Non-priority:** Consistency evaluation must not silently establish Policy priority or resolve Authority conflict.
- **P34-DC-063 — Integrity Trace:** Integrity claims must trace to Constitution, Authority, Approval, version, applicability, evidence, exception, and lifecycle.
- **P34-DC-064 — Conflict Escalation:** Unresolved Policy Conflict must be communicated to the eligible Governance, Human, organizational, or Director boundary without autonomous resolution.
