# Constitutional Principles

## Enterprise Constitution Supremacy

Every Program V phase remains subordinate to the Enterprise Constitution and Director authority.

## Separation of Responsibility

Identity, Trust, Permission, Capability, responsibility, accountability, Governance, Runtime, Execution, Security, and Director authority must retain distinct meanings.

## Least Authority

No Security identity, Trust finding, Permission, policy, control, observation, alert, response role, technical administrator, Runtime, or implementation may acquire authority beyond an explicit constitutional mandate.

## Human Architecture Preservation

Technical Security and Identity architecture must consume Human Architecture and must not redefine Human Identity, Relationships, Trust, Privacy, conversation boundaries, Memory eligibility, or approval authority.

## Governance–Authority Separation

Security Governance constrains and evaluates constitutional compatibility. It does not replace Director authority or make business decisions.

## Program Isolation

Program V must not absorb responsibilities assigned to another Program. Cross-Program dependency preserves identity, ownership, and authority.

## Enterprise-First Design

Security meaning, responsibility, authority, evidence, and boundaries precede technical realization.

## Technology Independence

No Program V definition may depend on a vendor, product, platform, protocol, framework, model, infrastructure service, deployment topology, database, interface, processor, or hardware architecture.

## Computational Independence

Security responsibilities remain stable across computing paradigms and execution-provider changes.

## Runtime Independence

Program V may govern Security boundaries applicable to Runtime, but it must not define Runtime identity, lifecycle, internal control, execution semantics, or implementation.

## Evidence and Traceability

Every material Security claim, constraint, finding, change, exception, escalation, and lifecycle decision must remain attributable and auditable.

## Fail-Closed Governance

Missing authority, ambiguous identity, unresolved constitutional conflict, absent evidence, failed dependency, or failed gate blocks architectural progression.

## Evolution Without Rewrite

Technology, threats, organizations, and realizations may evolve without silently rewriting Program V constitutional meaning.
