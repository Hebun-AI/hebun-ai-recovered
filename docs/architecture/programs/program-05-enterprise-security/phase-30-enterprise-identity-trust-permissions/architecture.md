# Phase 30 — Enterprise Identity, Trust & Permissions

## Canonical Status

**STATUS: COMPLETE / PUBLISHED**

This document defines the canonical Enterprise constitutional architecture of Identity, Trust, and Permission within Program V — Enterprise Security.

Phase 30 consumes published constitutional architecture as immutable input. It defines constitutional meaning, ownership, Governance, relationships, lifecycle, and boundaries only. It does not create accounts, authenticate participants, grant access, authorize execution, configure Security, or implement any mechanism.

Phase 30 is implementation independent, technology independent, computationally independent, Runtime independent, vendor independent, and AI-model independent.

---

# 1. Purpose

Enterprise Identity, Trust, and Permission exist to make participation and eligibility within Hebun AI:

- distinguishable without reducing identity to a technical account;
- contextual without merging identities across organizations or relationships;
- evidence-grounded without converting evidence into truth;
- explicitly governed without transferring Director authority;
- minimally permissive without assuming access from role, ownership, capability, or trust;
- attributable throughout constitutional lifecycle;
- consumable by Runtime without becoming Runtime state or implementation.

The mission of Phase 30 is to establish stable constitutional meaning before any future Security architecture constrains Runtime, AI, operations, policy, deployment, or resilience.

Identity answers **who or what is constitutionally recognized in a declared context**. Trust qualifies **the evidence-based fitness of a declared constitutional relationship between recognized Enterprise identities for a declared interaction**. Permission records **a bounded authorization eligibility applicable to a declared action or access class**. None independently creates constitutional authority or execution.

## Purpose Rules

- **P30-DC-001 — Meaning Before Mechanism:** Constitutional Identity, Trust, and Permission meaning must precede and remain independent from technical realization.
- **P30-DC-002 — Context Requirement:** Every Identity, Trust, and Permission expression must declare its applicable organizational, relationship, purpose, and lifecycle context.
- **P30-DC-003 — Minimal Eligibility:** No participant receives broader Permission or authority than explicitly required by an approved constitutional responsibility.
- **P30-DC-004 — Non-execution:** Phase 30 outputs must never constitute a command, Runtime dispatch, workflow transition, or execution authorization.

# 3. Core Principles

## Identity Uniqueness

Every Enterprise Identity must be uniquely distinguishable within its declared identity domain. Uniqueness does not require one global identity and does not merge a participant’s distinct contextual identities.

## Identity Persistence

Identity continuity must survive ordinary changes in participant attributes, implementation, technology, credentials, or Runtime realization. Persistence preserves history; it does not imply permanent eligibility.

## Identity Independence

Identity remains distinct from Trust, Permission, Authority, Capability, responsibility, ownership, account, credential, and Runtime representation.

## Trust Separation

Trust is contextual and evidence-based. It is not Identity, Permission, truth, confidence, reputation, approval, Authority, or technical access.

## Permission Minimality

Permission must be explicit, necessary, proportionate, Scope-bound, purpose-bound, time-aware, reviewable, and revocable.

## Least Authority

Permission may constrain the use of existing authority but cannot originate or amplify constitutional authority.

## Explicit Authorization

No silence, inference, organizational status, technical capability, ownership, trust, credential possession, or prior access constitutes authorization.

## Traceability

Identity recognition, Trust qualification, Permission eligibility, change, restriction, inheritance, revocation, exception, and Governance disposition must remain attributable.

## Accountability

Every material lifecycle act must identify the constitutionally responsible and accountable context. Technical operation never absorbs constitutional accountability.

## Non-transferability

Identity, Trust, Permission, and Authority do not transfer implicitly between people, systems, organizations, Tenants, relationships, roles, contexts, or implementations.

## Separation of Identity and Authority

Recognition answers who or what is present. Authority answers what normative mandate applies. Recognition alone creates no authority.

## Fail-Closed Interpretation

Missing or ambiguous Identity, Trust, Permission, Authority, Scope, evidence, ownership, accountability, lifecycle, or dependency blocks eligibility.

## Actor Neutrality

Human, organizational, AI, and System identities are governed by the same separation, evidence, traceability, and authority principles while retaining distinct identity categories.

## Evolution Without Rewrite

Identity representations, evidence, Trust qualifications, and Permissions may evolve through governed lifecycle without rewriting their canonical meanings or erasing history.

# 4. Canonical Definitions

## Enterprise Identity

> **Enterprise Identity is the persistent, attributable, context-bounded constitutional recognition of a distinguishable human, organization, AI participant, or system as a declared participant or subject within Hebun AI, independent from technical accounts, credentials, Trust, Permission, Authority, Capability, and Runtime representation.**

## Human Identity

> **Human Identity is the constitutional representation of how a person participates in a declared organizational and relationship context, as governed by Human Architecture and consumed by Phase 30 without redefinition.**

One person may hold multiple contextual Human Identities. They remain distinct and do not merge authority.

## Organizational Identity

> **Organizational Identity is the attributable constitutional recognition of an Organization or organizational unit within its declared Scope, ownership, responsibility, accountability, Tenant, and Governance boundaries.**

Organizational Identity does not convert membership, ownership, or hierarchy into personal Permission.

## AI Identity

> **AI Identity is the attributable constitutional recognition of an AI participant within a declared role, capability, responsibility, authority limit, organizational context, lifecycle, and Human oversight boundary.**

AI Identity does not imply agency, autonomy, personhood, Trust, Permission, or execution eligibility.

## System Identity

> **System Identity is the attributable constitutional recognition of a technical or Runtime participant as a bounded system subject within a declared organizational, responsibility, lifecycle, and authority context.**

System Identity is not an implementation identifier, account, process, deployment, service credential, or infrastructure definition.

## Trust

> **Trust is a contextual, evidence-based qualification of relationship depth and interaction eligibility, bounded to the recognized identities participating in that declared relationship and use.**

Trust is not authentication, truth, confidence, reputation, Permission, approval, Authority, ownership, or access.

## Permission

> **Permission is an explicit, minimal, attributable, Scope-bound, purpose-bound, lifecycle-governed constitutional eligibility for a recognized Identity to request or participate in a declared class of access or action under existing Authority and Governance.**

Permission is not Authority, Capability, responsibility, approval, execution eligibility, execution, or implementation configuration.

## Authority

> **Authority is the recognized, scoped, and revocable constitutional basis for normative force, originating from Director-governed architecture and valid delegation rather than from Identity, Trust, Permission, ownership, capability, or technical control.**

## Ownership

> **Ownership is the constitutionally recognized accountability relationship through which an identified owner remains answerable for the integrity, stewardship, and governed disposition of an enterprise object or outcome within a declared Scope.**

Ownership does not automatically grant Permission, Authority, universal access, truth, or execution eligibility.

## Accountability

> **Accountability is the persistent constitutional obligation of an identified responsible context to answer for an assigned responsibility, its evidence, its governed disposition, and its outcomes without transferring that obligation to a tool, Runtime, or implementation.**

## Authentication

> **Authentication is the constitutional qualification that a claimed Identity has been verified to the evidence threshold required for a declared context and use.**

Authentication is not a protocol, login, technical mechanism, Trust, Permission, Authority, or approval.

## Authorization

> **Authorization is the explicit governed determination that a declared Permission is applicable to a verified Identity, requested use, Scope, purpose, lifecycle interval, and existing Authority context.**

Authorization is not an algorithm, technical enforcement, Director Authority, execution, or permanent eligibility.

## Verification

> **Verification is the attributable evaluation of identity or eligibility evidence against declared canonical criteria without changing the evidence, fabricating missing evidence, or creating Authority.**

## Credential

> **Credential is attributable evidence or a governed evidence reference offered to support Identity verification within a declared context.**

A Credential is not Identity, Trust, Permission, Authority, access, a secret, a key, a certificate design, or a technology specification.

## Delegation Identity

> **Delegation Identity is the attributable constitutional reference that connects a recognized delegating context, delegate context, delegated responsibility, retained accountability, authority boundary, Scope, lifecycle, and provenance without redefining Enterprise Delegation.**

## Responsibility Identity

> **Responsibility Identity is the persistent, attributable constitutional reference by which a declared organizational obligation remains distinguishable across ownership, Delegation, Responsibility Handoff, Coordination, lifecycle, and accountability contexts.**

These definitions are authoritative for Phase 30 and immutable except through constitutional amendment. Similar technical or organizational terms must not silently replace them.

## Definition Rules

- **P30-DC-009 — Unique Contextual Identity:** Every Enterprise Identity must be distinguishable within its declared identity domain.
- **P30-DC-010 — No Global Merge:** Multiple contextual identities must not be silently merged into one authority-bearing identity.
- **P30-DC-011 — Category Preservation:** Human, Organizational, AI, and System Identity categories must remain distinguishable.
- **P30-DC-012 — Authentication Separation:** Authentication qualifies an Identity claim; it does not create Trust, Permission, or Authority.
- **P30-DC-013 — Authorization Separation:** Authorization qualifies Permission applicability; it does not create constitutional Authority or execute action.
- **P30-DC-014 — Credential Separation:** Credential evidence must never be treated as Identity, Trust, Permission, Authority, or access itself.
- **P30-DC-015 — Delegation Preservation:** Delegation Identity must reference and preserve published Enterprise Delegation without redefining it.
- **P30-DC-016 — Responsibility Continuity:** Responsibility Identity must preserve attributable responsibility and accountability across contextual change.

# 5. Architectural Relationships

The required constitutional relationship is:

```text
Recognized Identity
        ↓ qualifies the subject
Contextual Trust
        ↓ informs eligibility assessment
Explicit Permission
        ↓ is valid only under existing
Constitutional Authority
        ↓ may contribute to
Execution Eligibility
```

This chain is conditional, not generative:

1. Identity is required to attribute Trust and Permission, but Identity does not create either.
2. Trust may inform whether Permission is eligible, but Trust does not grant Permission.
3. Permission is valid only within applicable Authority; Permission does not create or expand Authority.
4. Authority establishes normative limits but does not by itself approve or execute work.
5. Execution Eligibility requires all applicable upstream conditions and later Runtime Security constraints; it is not execution.
6. Failure or ambiguity at any link blocks progression and remains visible.

The relationship must preserve:

- exact Identity and context;
- evidence and provenance;
- Scope and purpose;
- applicable organization and Tenant;
- Trust basis and limits;
- Permission identity and lifecycle;
- Authority source and delegation;
- ownership, responsibility, and accountability;
- Governance disposition;
- unresolved conflict or missing evidence.

## Relationship Rules

- **P30-DC-017 — Identity Non-conferment:** Identity recognition must not confer Trust, Permission, Authority, approval, or execution eligibility.
- **P30-DC-018 — Trust Non-conferment:** Trust must not grant Permission, Authority, approval, or execution eligibility.
- **P30-DC-019 — Permission Non-authority:** Permission must not originate, amplify, or replace constitutional Authority.
- **P30-DC-020 — Authority Non-execution:** Authority must not be interpreted as execution or as sufficient execution eligibility.
- **P30-DC-021 — Eligibility Non-execution:** Execution Eligibility is a governed precondition, not a command or execution.
- **P30-DC-022 — Directional Integrity:** Informational feedback must not reverse the canonical qualification direction or redefine an upstream identity.
- **P30-DC-023 — Fail-closed Chain:** Missing, expired, revoked, ambiguous, or conflicting qualification at any link blocks eligibility.

# 6. Identity Architecture

## Identity Creation

Creation is the governed admission of a distinguishable Identity into a declared constitutional context. It requires:

- an attributable subject;
- an authorized identity domain and accountable owner;
- declared Identity category;
- organization, Tenant, relationship, and Scope boundaries;
- evidence and provenance;
- responsibility and authority limits;
- lifecycle basis;
- conflict and duplicate evaluation.

Creation does not create a person, Organization, AI, System, account, Permission, Trust, or Authority.

## Identity Recognition

Recognition establishes that a previously created Identity is the applicable constitutional reference for a declared interaction. Recognition requires sufficient verification and must preserve uncertainty or conflict.

## Identity Persistence

Persistence preserves identity continuity, provenance, history, and attribution across changes in attributes, credentials, participants, implementations, or Runtime representations.

## Identity Evolution

Evolution is an attributable, reviewable change to allowed Identity attributes or context. It must preserve prior versions and must not silently merge identities, transfer Authority, or erase accountability.

## Identity Retirement

Retirement ends future active use of an Identity in a declared context while preserving history, provenance, accountability, and required references. Retirement does not erase prior acts or automatically retire responsibilities, Permissions, or records; each dependent lifecycle must be governed explicitly.

## Identity Responsibilities

Constitutional Identity stewardship must:

- preserve uniqueness and contextual separation;
- maintain an accountable owner or steward;
- protect Human Architecture and Tenant boundaries;
- prevent collision, impersonation, silent merge, and unauthorized transfer;
- keep current lifecycle and verification status visible;
- retain provenance and audit history;
- escalate ambiguity or conflict.

## Identity Lifecycle Rules

- **P30-DC-024 — Governed Creation:** Identity creation requires attributable evidence, authorized Scope, category, ownership, and lifecycle.
- **P30-DC-025 — Recognition Threshold:** Identity recognition requires evidence appropriate to the declared context and use.
- **P30-DC-026 — Persistent History:** Identity evolution and retirement must preserve prior identity history and attribution.
- **P30-DC-027 — No Silent Merge:** Identity collision or ambiguity must be resolved through Governance, never silent consolidation.
- **P30-DC-028 — Retirement Preservation:** Retirement must block unauthorized future use without erasing historical responsibility or accountability.
- **P30-DC-029 — Dependent Lifecycle Separation:** Identity lifecycle change must not silently change Trust, Permission, Authority, ownership, or responsibility lifecycle.

# 7. Trust Architecture

## Constitutional Trust

Trust is always attached to:

- a declared constitutional relationship among recognized identities;
- a declared interaction or use;
- an organization, Tenant, relationship, Scope, and purpose;
- attributable evidence;
- an evaluation time and lifecycle;
- explicit limits and uncertainty;
- an accountable Governance context.

No universal Trust score exists constitutionally.

## Trust Establishment

Trust may be established only from sufficient attributable evidence, verified contextual continuity, applicable policy, and authorized review. Usage volume, confidence, technical access, payment, seniority, popularity, or elapsed time is insufficient.

## Trust Preservation

Trust preservation requires continuing validity of Identity, relationship, evidence, purpose, boundaries, and Governance conditions. Preservation is not permanence.

## Trust Degradation

Trust may be qualified, reduced, restricted, suspended, or marked uncertain when evidence weakens, contradictions appear, context changes, obligations fail, or applicability cannot be confirmed.

## Trust Revocation

Trust must be revocable. Revocation ends its future applicability in the declared context while preserving evidence, reasons, history, affected dependencies, and accountability.

## Trust Rules

- **P30-DC-030 — Contextual Trust:** Trust must be bounded to the exact recognized identities, relationship, organization, Tenant, Scope, purpose, and use.
- **P30-DC-031 — Evidence-grounded Trust:** Trust requires attributable evidence and must expose uncertainty and contradiction.
- **P30-DC-032 — No Automatic Inheritance:** Trust must not transfer automatically across identities, relationships, roles, organizations, Tenants, purposes, or time.
- **P30-DC-033 — Trust Lifecycle:** Establishment, preservation, degradation, suspension, and revocation must remain attributable and reviewable.
- **P30-DC-034 — Trust–Authentication Separation:** Successful Authentication must not be treated as Trust.
- **P30-DC-035 — Trust Non-authority:** Trust must not become Permission, Authority, approval, ownership, or execution eligibility.

# 8. Permission Architecture

## Permission Identity

Every Permission must have a distinguishable constitutional identity that declares:

- eligible Identity;
- permitted access or action class;
- applicable object, domain, or boundary;
- purpose and Scope;
- Authority source;
- issuing and accountable context;
- constraints and exclusions;
- effective interval and review condition;
- provenance and evidence;
- lifecycle status.

## Permission Ownership

Permission ownership is accountability for the integrity and governed lifecycle of the Permission expression. It does not grant the owner that Permission or transfer Authority.

## Permission Lifecycle

A Permission may be proposed, reviewed, explicitly authorized, active, restricted, suspended, expired, revoked, or retired. Lifecycle status must remain explicit. Technical use or prior use does not activate or renew Permission.

## Permission Inheritance

Permission inheritance is prohibited by default. Any constitutionally permitted inheritance must be:

- explicit;
- minimal;
- attributable;
- Scope-, purpose-, organization-, Tenant-, and time-bounded;
- derived from valid existing Authority;
- non-transitive unless separately authorized;
- reversible and reviewable;
- blocked when identity or context is ambiguous.

## Permission Revocation

Permission must be revocable. Revocation removes future eligibility within the declared Scope while preserving history, reasons, affected dependencies, and accountability. Revocation does not erase prior acts and does not itself execute remediation.

## Permission Rules

- **P30-DC-036 — Explicit Permission:** Permission must be explicitly attributable; silence, inference, prior use, technical possession, or organizational status is insufficient.
- **P30-DC-037 — Permission Minimality:** Permission must be no broader or longer than required by the approved responsibility and purpose.
- **P30-DC-038 — Permission Ownership Separation:** Owning or administering a Permission expression must not confer that Permission.
- **P30-DC-039 — Inheritance Denied by Default:** Permission inheritance is invalid unless explicitly authorized and bounded.
- **P30-DC-040 — No Transitive Permission:** Permission must not become transitive through role, relationship, delegation, group, organization, system, or implementation without separate constitutional authorization.
- **P30-DC-041 — Revocable Permission:** Every Permission must support governed restriction, suspension, expiration, revocation, and retirement.
- **P30-DC-042 — Permission–Execution Separation:** Active Permission must not be treated as approval, Runtime admission, execution eligibility, or execution.
- **P30-DC-043 — Lifecycle Traceability:** Every Permission lifecycle change must retain source, rationale, authority, evidence, time, actor, and prior state.
