# Executive Summary

Program VII — Computer Use is the Director-governed constitutional Program that establishes the authority and boundaries under which the Enterprise may interact with, execute upon, automate, and integrate with computer environments and external platforms.

The core constitutional risk Program VII governs is the collapse of distinct authorities into technical capability: the assumption that because the system *can* observe a surface, actuate a control, run a procedure, or reach an external platform, it is thereby *permitted* to do so, *approved* to do so, or *authoritative* in doing so. Program VII exists to keep these separated. Capability is not Permission. Interaction is not Authority. Execution is not Runtime authority. Automation is not Autonomy. Integration is not ownership of an external system.

Program VII owns the constitutional meaning of four assigned territories only — Computer Interaction, Governed Computer Execution, Enterprise Automation, and Enterprise Integration for Computer Use — and it consumes, without redefining, the approved Work, Security, Runtime, Reasoning, Governance, Human, Organization, and Director authority architecture established by Programs I–VI and the published Runtime Platform.

This Constitution defines the durable governance envelope for all future Program VII phases without defining those phases. It selects no technology, names no vendor, prescribes no implementation, and opens no phase.

# Mission

The mission of Program VII is to establish a coherent, safe, accountable, and constitutionally bounded Computer Use layer that:

- preserves Enterprise Constitution supremacy and Director authority;
- ensures every computer-mediated interaction, execution, automation, and integration remains subordinate to approved Work, Security, Runtime, and authority architecture;
- keeps capability strictly separated from Permission, and action strictly separated from Authority;
- preserves the boundary between constitutional architecture and operational Runtime realization;
- preserves Human review, Human Approval, Human Override, and Director-reserved authority over consequential computer-mediated action;

- provides stable architectural outputs consumable by separately governed downstream Programs.

Program VII succeeds through constitutional clarity, boundary preservation, safety, accountability, and durable meaning — not through automation breadth, tool coverage, integration count, model capability, throughput, or product behavior.

# Constitutional Principles

## Enterprise Constitution Supremacy

Every Program VII artifact remains subordinate to the Enterprise Constitution and Director authority.

## Human Primacy

Computer Use exists to serve eligible Human and organizational objectives. It must not erase Human agency, responsibility, review, Approval, or Override over consequential computer-mediated action.

## Director Authority Preservation

No interaction outcome, execution result, automation state, integration status, tool availability, technical feasibility, or capability may become Director authority or infer Director approval.

## Capability–Permission Separation

The ability to observe, actuate, execute, automate, or reach a system creates no Permission. Permission originates only from Program V Security and applicable Governance, never from technical possibility.

## Interaction–Authority Separation

Observing or interacting with a computer surface establishes no Authority over that surface, its data, its owning system, or the Enterprise decisions that surround it.

## Execution–Runtime Separation

Governed Computer Execution is a constitutional responsibility boundary. It does not become Runtime, does not grant Runtime admission, and does not redefine published Runtime identity, lifecycle, or operational governance.

## Automation–Autonomy Separation

Automation is bounded, attributable repetition of approved action. It is not autonomy, not self-authorization, not unbounded initiative, and not delegated Director authority.

## Integration–Ownership Separation

Integration establishes bounded, governed interaction with an external or enterprise system. It never transfers ownership of, authority over, or accountability for that system to Program VII.

## Approval-Before-Action

No consequential, irreversible, or externally observable computer-mediated action is eligible until the applicable Work, Security, Governance, Runtime, and authority boundaries have been satisfied through their own architecture.

## Program Isolation

Program VII must not absorb responsibilities assigned to another Program. Dependencies are directional and non-owning.

## Least Authority

Interaction reach, execution capability, automation scope, integration access, urgency, role, or technical feasibility creates no undeclared Authority or Permission.

## Safety and Reversibility Preference

Constitutional architecture for Computer Use must preserve the ability to bound, observe, constrain, and — where architecture requires — refuse, defer, or escalate consequential action. Ambiguity, missing authority, or missing safety context must not default to action.

## Traceability and Accountability

Every governed interaction, execution, automation, and integration must remain attributable, Scope-bound, and traceable to eligible authority, approval, and provenance.

## Fail-Closed Governance

Missing Identity, Authority, Scope, Permission, Policy, approval, Runtime admission, safety context, or required review blocks constitutional progression and requires restriction, deferral, or escalation.

## Technology and Vendor Independence

No Program VII meaning may depend on an operating system, device, interface, browser, protocol, tool, API, model, framework, platform, or vendor.

## Runtime and Implementation Independence

Program VII may define constitutional architecture consumable by Runtime, but it must not define Runtime behavior, execution engines, drivers, agents, tools, algorithms, services, infrastructure, deployment, or implementation.

## Evolution Without Rewrite

Operating systems, interfaces, protocols, tools, models, technologies, organizations, and implementations may evolve without silently rewriting Program VII constitutional meaning.
