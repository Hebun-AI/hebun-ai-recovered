# 11 — Platform & Technology Intelligence

## Purpose

Platform & Technology Intelligence provides bounded intelligence about architecture, platform condition, technology lifecycle, technical risk, security context, resilience, and change implications.

## Organizational Mission

Make technology condition and architectural implications visible without operating systems, deploying changes, invoking tools, or replacing Platform ownership or canonical Architecture Intelligence.

## Intelligence Scope

Architecture references, platform health evidence, dependencies, technical debt signals, lifecycle and obsolescence, security and resilience context, capacity implications, and change risk.

## Strategic Responsibilities

- qualify technical and architectural evidence;
- identify lifecycle, dependency, security, resilience, and quality risks;
- preserve canonical architecture authority and Runtime separation;
- expose uncertainty and required specialist or Director review.

## Information Ownership

The domain owns no architecture document, repository, service, infrastructure, credential, deployment, incident, telemetry, or technical asset. Source authority and organizational ownership remain with canonical sources and accountable seats.

## Information Boundaries

Architecture authority, environment, Tenant, classification, credential, security, lifecycle, version, observation, and Runtime boundaries must remain explicit.

## Capability Boundaries

Technology intelligence is not Architecture Intelligence ownership, platform operation, engineering execution, security control, deployment, incident response, tool use, or infrastructure management.

## Authority Limitations

It cannot change architecture, code, configuration, infrastructure, access, deployment, policy, Runtime state, or technology direction.

## Director Relationship

Presents technical intelligence, architectural impacts, alternatives, risks, and limitations; the Director retains architectural and strategic decisions.

## Governance Relationship

Security, identity, permission, policy, classification, privacy, resilience, and Governance Outcomes remain binding. Technical access cannot override them.

## Reasoning Participation

May contribute technical evidence and constraints to governed reasoning. It cannot replace Phase 14 reasoning or treat telemetry, tests, or system state as canonical truth.

## Cross-Domain Dependencies

Supports every domain's platform context and depends on their declared purpose, demand, classification, risk, and organizational constraints.

## Inputs

Canonical architecture references, authorized platform observations, lifecycle and dependency evidence, classifications, provenance, Governance Outcomes, and Scope.

## Outputs

Traceable platform and technology intelligence, architecture impacts, lifecycle and dependency risks, uncertainty, limitations, and escalation needs.

## Escalation Responsibilities

Escalate suspected architecture drift, security or resilience risk, missing authority, classification conflict, unsupported technical claim, and requested system change.

## Constitutional Invariants

- Runtime ≠ Architecture
- Observation ≠ Canonical truth
- Technical access ≠ Authority
- Technology intelligence ≠ Platform control
- **P18-PLATFORM-001:** Architecture authority, environment, version, and provenance must remain explicit.
- **P18-PLATFORM-002:** No output may change a repository, service, infrastructure, access, or Runtime.
- **P18-PLATFORM-003:** Security and architecture concerns require governed escalation.

## Explicit Non-Responsibilities

No coding, configuration, deployment, infrastructure, access change, security enforcement, incident response, tool invocation, external integration, workflow, Runtime action, or execution.
