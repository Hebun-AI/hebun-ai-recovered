# 09 — Agent Escalation Model

## Purpose

Escalation preserves authority when an Agent cannot safely continue within its participation envelope. It reports an unresolved condition to the accountable human or Director boundary; it is not a decision, delegation, workflow, or execution request.

## Escalation Conditions

An Agent must escalate:

- missing or conflicting canonical authority;
- material evidence insufficiency or contradiction;
- Scope ambiguity or attempted expansion;
- requested approval, decision, authorization, ownership, or execution;
- policy, compliance, privacy, legal, or governance uncertainty;
- suspected canonical drift or integrity failure;
- conflict between Agent findings that requires authoritative judgment;
- inability to preserve provenance or constitutional boundaries.

## Escalation Content

An escalation must identify the Agent and archetype, participation Scope, exact unresolved question, evidence references, known conflicts, uncertainty, prohibited continuation, appropriate recipient, and whether participation has ended or is suspended.

## Recipients

The recipient is the accountable human-review boundary or the Director when strategy, canonical architecture, governance exception, final decision, approval, ownership, roadmap, or execution authorization is involved.

## Rules

- **P17-ESCALATION-001:** Escalation must preserve the unresolved question without proposing implicit approval.
- **P17-ESCALATION-002:** Escalation must not create or transfer authority.
- **P17-ESCALATION-003:** Participation must stop when safe continuation depends on the escalation outcome.
- **P17-ESCALATION-004:** Absence of response must not be interpreted as consent.
- **P17-ESCALATION-005:** An Agent must not resolve its own authority conflict.
- **P17-ESCALATION-006:** Escalation evidence must remain attributable and immutable.

## Boundary

No notification transport, queue, priority algorithm, routing service, SLA, state machine, or escalation workflow is defined.
