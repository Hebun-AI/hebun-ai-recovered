# 03 — Director Model

## Purpose

The Director Model defines responsibilities that remain reserved to the Director regardless of Agent capability, confidence, specialization, number, or maturity.

## Reserved Responsibilities

The Director owns:

- enterprise strategy and organizational direction;
- priorities and acceptable trade-offs;
- canonical architecture approval;
- governance and exception authority;
- final decisions and overrides;
- approval of committing actions;
- execution authorization;
- assignment of accountable organizational ownership.

An Agent may prepare information relevant to these responsibilities but cannot inherit, aggregate, infer, simulate, or delegate them.

## Relationship to Agents

The Director admits bounded participation, establishes its Scope, considers its outputs, requests human review where required, and may suspend or terminate participation. Agent confidence, consensus, persistence, expertise, or historical acceptance does not constrain Director judgment.

## Human-in-the-Loop

Human involvement is mandatory whenever canonical authority, unresolved conflict, material uncertainty, policy exception, approval, organizational ownership, roadmap change, or execution authorization is implicated. Human review supports the Director; it does not create a parallel authority source.

## Rules

- **P17-DIRECTOR-001:** The Director remains final authority for every canonical or committing decision.
- **P17-DIRECTOR-002:** No Agent output may be interpreted as implicit Director approval.
- **P17-DIRECTOR-003:** Repeated acceptance of Agent advice must not create delegated authority by precedent.
- **P17-DIRECTOR-004:** Only an explicit governed act may record a Director decision or authorization.
- **P17-DIRECTOR-005:** Agent suspension, retirement, or replacement must not diminish Director authority.

## Enterprise Example

Several architecture specialists may agree that a boundary should change. Their agreement is evidence for review, not a decision. The Director may accept, reject, defer, or override it.
