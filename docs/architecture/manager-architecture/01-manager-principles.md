# 01 — Manager Principles

## Purpose

The Manager Principles are the constitution of a Manager — the commitments any Manager must obey. A Manager is the authority seat that governs a department; these principles are what keep that governance bounded, accountable, and always subordinate to the department, the enterprise, and the Director. Any Manager that violates one of these is not doing Manager Architecture.

## Architectural role

These principles constrain all the Manager topics that follow (model, authority, delegation, oversight, escalation, governance). Every subsequent document inherits them first. They keep a Manager a governor — not an engine — bounded, authority-preserving, governed, and traceable.

## The principles

### 1. A Manager governs; it does not perform work
A Manager owns, delegates, oversees, reports, and escalates for a department. It performs no reasoning ([Director Intelligence](../director-reasoning/README.md)), no work ([Execution](../director-execution/README.md)), and no orchestration of running work ([execution orchestration](../execution-orchestration/README.md)). Governing and doing are different concerns; a Manager holds only the first.

### 2. A Manager's authority is delegated and revocable
A Manager holds only the authority delegated to it, and that delegation is always revocable ([enterprise authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)). A Manager never originates authority and never exceeds its grant. Director Authority sits above every Manager.

### 3. A Manager governs exactly one department
A Manager is the authority seat of one department and governs that department's domain only ([department architecture](../department-architecture/README.md)). Its governance does not reach into another department. One Manager seat, one department.

### 4. A Manager carries the department's ownership and accountability
A Manager holds the department's ownership of its domain and answers for that domain upward — to the enterprise level and ultimately the Director ([department responsibilities](../department-architecture/03-department-responsibilities.md)). Ownership without upward accountability is never permitted.

### 5. Delegation is downward and bounded
A Manager delegates bounded authority to the specialist seats beneath it, never more than it holds ([delegation model](04-delegation-model.md)). Delegation narrows as it descends; a Manager cannot delegate an authority it was not itself granted.

### 6. A Manager oversees and reports honestly
A Manager oversees the work it has delegated and reports progress, status, and problems upward truthfully ([oversight & reporting](05-oversight-and-reporting.md)). It does not hide a failure or overstate results. Honest reporting is what lets the enterprise trust and steer a department.

### 7. A Manager escalates what exceeds its authority
When a matter exceeds a Manager's delegated authority, the Manager escalates upward rather than acting beyond its grant ([escalation & governance](06-escalation-governance.md)). Escalation, not overreach, is the correct response to a limit.

### 8. A Manager is governed and traceable
Every Manager falls under the enterprise-wide governance regime, and its authority, delegations, oversight, reports, and escalations can be reconstructed and audited ([escalation & governance](06-escalation-governance.md)). No Manager is outside governance; none is exempt from traceability.

### 9. A Manager never reasons, plans, or decides in place of Director Intelligence
When its department needs reasoning, planning, or a decision, a Manager routes it to Director Intelligence; it produces none and alters none ([manager boundaries in the model](02-manager-model.md)). A Manager governs the outcome's ownership — never the reasoning, plan, or decision itself.

## Inputs

- The **Enterprise Organization and Department architectures** ([9A](../enterprise-organization/README.md), [9B](../department-architecture/README.md)) — the hierarchy, department, and authority a Manager governs within.
- **Delegated authority** — the bounded, revocable grant a Manager holds.

## Outputs

- A **principled frame** every Manager topic operates within — the standard any Manager is held to.

## Boundaries

- These principles **define no concrete manager, department, or specialist** — they state what a Manager must obey, not who fills the seat.
- They **describe no runtime or mechanism** — manager machinery is a later phase behind the Director gate.

## Future direction

Future concrete managers — human or AI — will still obey these principles: govern not perform, delegated authority, one department, carry ownership and accountability, delegate downward bounded, oversee and report honestly, escalate at the limit, governed and traceable, never reason/plan/decide in place of Director Intelligence. Managers become real; the constitution holds.
