# 05 — Oversight & Reporting

## Purpose

Oversight & Reporting defines **how a Manager oversees the work it has delegated and reports upward**. A Manager governs a department not by doing its work but by watching it and keeping the levels above informed. This document defines the Manager's oversight duty (looking down at delegated work) and reporting duty (speaking up to those it answers to).

## Architectural role

Where delegation ([04](04-delegation-model.md)) hands authority down, oversight watches what was delegated and reporting carries what oversight finds back up. Escalation ([06](06-escalation-governance.md)) is the sharp end of reporting — what a Manager raises when a matter exceeds its grant. Together, delegation, oversight, reporting, and escalation are how a Manager governs without performing.

## The model — oversight

### A Manager oversees; it does not perform
Oversight means watching the delegated work stay within its bounds and its domain — not doing the work, and not orchestrating it ([execution orchestration](../execution-orchestration/README.md)). A Manager observes and holds accountable; it does not step in and execute ([manager principles](01-manager-principles.md)).

### Oversight covers what was delegated
A Manager oversees the specialist seats it delegated authority to, within its department. It does not oversee another department's work or reach beyond its delegation reach ([delegation model](04-delegation-model.md)). Oversight matches delegation.

### Oversight preserves accountability
Because a Manager still owns and answers for the department's outcomes ([manager principles](01-manager-principles.md)), oversight is how it keeps that accountability real — it watches enough to answer honestly for what its department produced.

## The model — reporting

### A Manager reports upward, honestly
A Manager reports the department's status, progress, and problems to the level above it and, through it, toward the Director ([enterprise organizational model](../enterprise-organization/02-organizational-model.md)). Reporting is truthful — a Manager does not hide a failure, overstate progress, or claim an outcome that did not happen ([manager principles](01-manager-principles.md)). Honest reporting is what makes a department governable from above.

### Reporting is traceable
What a Manager reports, and when, can be reconstructed and audited ([escalation & governance](06-escalation-governance.md)). Reporting is part of the department's traceable record, not an informal aside.

### Reporting is not deciding
A Manager reports facts and status upward; it does not decide the enterprise's response. Decisions belong to Director Intelligence under Director Authority ([decision](../director-decision/README.md)). A Manager surfaces; the Director's intelligence decides.

## Inputs

- The **delegated work** ([delegation model](04-delegation-model.md)) — what oversight watches.
- The **accountability line** ([manager model](02-manager-model.md)) — where reports go.

## Outputs

- **Oversight** — a Manager's held-accountable view of its delegated work.
- **Reports** — honest, traceable status carried upward toward the Director.

## Boundaries

- Oversees and reports on **seats and their work, not concrete occupants** — it names none.
- Performs **no work and no orchestration** — oversight watches; it does not do or coordinate.
- Makes **no decisions** — reporting surfaces; it does not resolve.
- Describes **no runtime or mechanism** — only the structure of oversight and reporting.

## Future direction

As human and AI managers fill the seat and specialists fill the seats below, oversight and reporting scale with them — more delegated work to watch, more status to carry up — under the same rules: oversee not perform, report honestly, stay traceable, never decide. The department grows; it stays visible and accountable from above.
