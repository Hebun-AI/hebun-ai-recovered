# 01 — Reasoning Philosophy

## Reasoning transforms organizational knowledge into organizational judgment

This is the whole of Director Reasoning in one sentence. The layers beneath it hold **knowledge** — what exists (the model), how it connects (the graph), what has happened (memory). Knowledge is necessary but inert: it describes the organization; it does not decide anything. Reasoning is the layer that takes that knowledge and produces **judgment** — an understanding of what matters, what to do, and why.

Knowledge answers *"what is true?"* Reasoning answers *"so what should we make of it?"* The transform between them is the reason this layer exists.

## Why the transform is needed

An organization can accumulate perfect knowledge and still be unable to act well. Facts do not rank themselves; connections do not weigh their own importance; history does not draw its own lessons. Someone — or something — must interpret the knowledge into a judgment a decision can rest on.

Without a reasoning layer:

- Knowledge stays a reference, not a guide. The organization can *look things up* but not *figure things out*.
- Every decision starts from raw facts, re-deriving understanding each time.
- The organization's memory, however rich, never becomes wisdom — the memory → experience → wisdom progression ([Phase 6](../memory/01-memory-philosophy.md)) stalls at memory.

Director Reasoning is what carries the organization across that gap: from *having* knowledge to *understanding* it, and from understanding to sound recommendation.

## What "judgment" means here

Judgment is not a decision executed — it is a decision *reasoned to*. It is:

- **Interpretation** — reading the facts, connections, and history together to see what they mean.
- **Weighing** — ranking what matters, trading off competing goods, evaluating opportunity and risk.
- **Conclusion** — arriving at a recommendation, a plan, or a decision, with its reasoning attached.

Judgment is the *output* of reasoning and the *input* to the Director's authority. Reasoning forms the judgment; the Director owns whether to act on it ([05 — Director Authority](05-director-authority.md)).

## The character of Hebun's reasoning

Because Hebun reasons over an organization's own model, graph, and memory, its judgment is:

- **Grounded** — every judgment traces to evidence in the knowledge layers, never to invention.
- **Contextual** — the same fact is judged differently depending on the organizational and historical context around it.
- **Continuous with the past** — reasoning draws on memory, so judgment compounds on experience rather than starting fresh.
- **Explainable** — because the judgment is built from traceable knowledge, it can always be accounted for.

## Reasoning is a discipline, not a mood

The rest of this directory ([02 First Principles](02-first-principles.md), [04 Decision Principles](04-decision-principles.md)) exists because judgment must be *disciplined* to be trustworthy. Reasoning that cut corners — reaching conclusions ahead of evidence, optimizing a part against the whole, hiding its uncertainty — would produce confident bad judgment, which is worse than none. The philosophy of Director Reasoning is that turning knowledge into judgment is a serious act, governed by principles, always accountable, and always subordinate to the Director's authority.
