# 05 — Director Authority

The authority principle of Director Reasoning, and the foundation of the entire layer: **the Director always owns final authority.** Reasoning advises; the Director decides. This is not a constraint bolted onto reasoning — it is the reason reasoning is safe to build.

## The Director always owns final authority

Every judgment reasoning produces is a **recommendation to the Director**, never a decision imposed on the organization. The authority to accept, reject, defer, or override a recommendation rests entirely and permanently with the Director. Reasoning is the counsel; the Director is the decision-maker. That ordering never inverts.

This holds no matter how good the reasoning becomes. Stronger reasoning produces better recommendations — it never earns authority. Capability and authority are separate: reasoning's capability may grow without bound; its authority stays at zero.

## Reasoning never overrides the Director

- **A recommendation is not a decision.** Reasoning concluding that X is best does not make X happen, and does not obligate the Director to X. The Director may decide otherwise, for reasons reasoning does not have.
- **Disagreement resolves to the Director.** When reasoning's judgment and the Director's choice differ, the Director's choice governs. Reasoning may state its case — honestly and fully, per [truth over convenience](02-first-principles.md) — but it does not get the last word. The Director does.
- **No standing mandate.** Reasoning holds no blanket authorization to act on categories of decision. Authority is exercised by the Director, decision by decision.

## Recommendations require explicit approval before irreversible actions

The hardest edge of the authority principle:

- **Reversible, internal reasoning is free.** Forming judgments, ranking options, preparing recommendations — all happen without approval, because none of it acts on the world ([03 — Reasoning Boundaries](03-reasoning-boundaries.md)).
- **Irreversible or committing actions require explicit Director approval.** Anything that reaches outward, spends, commits, or cannot be taken back proceeds *only* after the Director explicitly approves it. Reasoning prepares such actions completely; it never triggers them.
- **Approval is explicit and per-action.** It is given by the Director, for a specific action, in the moment. It is not inferred from a recommendation's strength, not implied by past approvals, and not granted standing. Each irreversible action is its own approval.

This mirrors the Director Gate discipline that governs the whole platform ([hebun-loop](../../architecture-backlog/00-capability-lifecycle.md)) and the Personal Enterprise Mode gates ([capability](../capabilities/personal-enterprise-mode/03-director-gates.md)) — reasoning is held to the same authority boundary as everything else Hebun does.

## Why authority stays with the Director

- **Accountability lives with authority.** The Director owns the organization and its consequences; the one who bears the outcome must be the one who decides. Reasoning bears no consequences and therefore holds no authority.
- **Explainability serves authority.** Reasoning is required to be explainable ([first principles](02-first-principles.md)) precisely so the Director can *own* the decision — interrogate the recommendation, and accept or reject it knowingly. Advice you cannot examine is authority quietly transferred; explainability keeps it with the Director.
- **Trust requires it.** An organization can trust a reasoning layer of enormous capability only because that layer cannot decide for it. The boundary is what makes the power safe.

## The principle, once more

Reasoning makes the Director's decisions **better**. It never makes them **instead**. However capable Hebun's reasoning grows, the Director remains the source of authority, the decision-maker, and the only one who can commit the organization to an irreversible act. That fixed point is the ground the whole cognitive layer stands on.
