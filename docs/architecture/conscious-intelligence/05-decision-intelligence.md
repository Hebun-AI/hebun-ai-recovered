# 05 — Decision Intelligence

## Definition

Decision Intelligence preserves the rationale and outcomes of important decisions so future reflection can learn from evidence without replacing human judgment.

## Decision Record

Every important decision record includes:

- objective;
- owner and decision authority;
- decision time and review dates;
- assumptions;
- alternatives considered;
- constraints and values implicated;
- expected outcome;
- actual outcome when known;
- supporting evidence;
- confidence at decision time and review time;
- lessons learned;
- related decisions, projects, events, and memories;
- version, annotations, and review status.

## Lifecycle

```text
Proposed → Decided → In Effect → Outcome Observed
         → Reviewed → Superseded or Closed
```

The historical decision is immutable. Later reviews, corrected assumptions, actual outcomes, and lessons are appended and linked.

## Intelligence Capabilities

- reconstruct decision history;
- compare expected and actual outcomes;
- identify recurring assumption failures;
- identify success patterns with confidence limits;
- distinguish correlation from causation;
- schedule conceptual review obligations;
- produce future recommendations as options.

## Safeguards

Patterns must have adequate coverage and provenance. “Recurring mistake” language must describe a decision pattern, not attack a person. Outcome quality must be evaluated against the original objective and context, not hindsight alone.

## Enterprise Example

Three hiring decisions shared an assumption about time-to-productivity. Outcome reviews show that the assumption was repeatedly optimistic. The system surfaces the pattern and supporting records, but the next hiring decision remains human-owned.

## Boundaries

Decision history ≠ decision authority, outcome ≠ personal worth, pattern ≠ certainty, lesson ≠ rule, and recommendation ≠ decision.
