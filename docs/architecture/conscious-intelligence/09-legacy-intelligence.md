# 09 — Legacy Intelligence

## Definition

Legacy Intelligence preserves user- or organization-approved knowledge so ideas, writings, decisions, inventions, research, lessons, and history remain attributable, understandable, and retrievable in the future.

Legacy is a preservation scope, not a claim of immortality, identity continuation, or a digital person.

## Supported Content

- ideas and conceptual notes;
- writings, books, and journals;
- business and architectural decisions;
- company history;
- inventions and research;
- lessons learned;
- user-approved personal reflections;
- provenance and contextual evidence.

## Preservation Model

Every legacy item must retain:

- creator, contributor, and owner attribution;
- source and evidence;
- creation and publication time;
- scope and intended audience;
- sensitivity and access conditions;
- version and evolution chain;
- intellectual-property status;
- retention and succession instructions;
- authenticity and integrity status;
- user-defined future retrieval conditions.

## Evolution Tracking

New versions and interpretations link to originals. They do not overwrite earlier records. A future reader must be able to distinguish:

- original content;
- correction;
- revision;
- commentary;
- derived summary;
- translation;
- later interpretation.

## Succession and Access

Future access must follow explicit user or organizational instructions, applicable law, privacy rights of other people, and security governance. Silence must never be interpreted as consent to publish.

## Boundaries

Legacy Intelligence must not generate posthumous speech as though it were the person, infer unapproved final wishes, expose third-party private information, or claim that preserved material constitutes a continuing identity.
