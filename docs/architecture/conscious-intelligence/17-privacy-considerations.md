# 17 — Privacy Considerations

## Privacy Objective

Long-term continuity must preserve human dignity, contextual integrity, and control. The ability to remember more does not create permission to retain or infer more.

## Governing Principles

- informed and specific consent;
- purpose limitation;
- data minimization;
- sensitivity-aware defaults;
- limited retention;
- user access and intelligibility;
- correction through governed supersession;
- revocation of future use;
- portability and export;
- deletion where legally required;
- third-party privacy;
- separation of personal and organizational authority;
- no secondary use without renewed authorization.

## Consent Model

Consent must identify:

- subject and owner;
- information class;
- allowed purposes;
- allowed domains;
- permitted recipients;
- retention period;
- whether derived analysis is allowed;
- whether consolidation is allowed;
- review date;
- withdrawal method.

Bundled, ambiguous, inferred, or perpetual consent is invalid for sensitive permanent memory.

## Third-party Context

Relationship and conversation memories often concern people who are not the primary user. The system must minimize third-party content, distinguish the user's account from shared truth, restrict disclosure, and avoid inferred profiles. One person's consent cannot authorize another person's sensitive-data exploitation.

## Sensitive Domains

Health, emotional balance, faith, spirituality, family, intimate relationships, financial information, journals, and legacy instructions require explicit opt-in, narrowly defined purpose, restricted access, shorter review cycles, and stronger disclosure controls.

## Revocation and Deletion

Revocation removes eligibility for future use and propagates to continuity packages, derived summaries, and active analyses. Derived outputs must be invalidated or recomputed when their basis is revoked.

Where law or policy requires deletion, content is removed through a governed process while preserving only the minimal non-content proof necessary for compliance. This exception must not become a mechanism for covert retention.

## Privacy Explanations

Users must be able to understand:

- what is retained;
- why it is retained;
- where it came from;
- how it has been used;
- what was inferred;
- who can access it;
- when it expires;
- how to correct, revoke, archive, export, or delete it.

## Boundaries

Personalization ≠ unlimited consent, continuity ≠ perpetual retention, user benefit ≠ lawful basis, and de-identification ≠ zero re-identification risk.
