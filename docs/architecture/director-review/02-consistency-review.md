# 02 — Consistency Review

Verifies that the seven Phase 7 design bodies describe **one coherent reasoning architecture**, not seven overlapping ones.

## The seven bodies and their roles

| Body | Answers |
|---|---|
| 7A Philosophy | *Why* the Director reasons |
| 7B Cognition | *How* the Director thinks (the lifecycle) |
| 7C Mechanisms | *What cognitive tools* realize the thinking |
| 7D Planning | *How* approved reasoning becomes a plan |
| 7E Decision | *How* validated plans become a decision |
| 7F Verification | *Whether* the chain holds up, independently |
| 7G Orchestration | *How* all of it runs as one workflow |

Seven layered views of one intelligence: philosophy grounds cognition; cognition uses mechanisms; planning, decision, and verification act on cognition's output; orchestration coordinates all of them. Consistency means the same commitments hold across all seven.

## Consistency checks

### Advisory-only, produces judgment not action
Stated as a 7A principle ([reasoning boundaries](../director-reasoning/03-reasoning-boundaries.md)) and upheld by every later body: planning prepares but never executes, decision produces decision-ready outcomes, verification critiques, orchestration coordinates — none acts. **Consistent across all seven.**

### Director Authority
Every body ends its chain at the Director: reasoning at the Director Gate, planning gated for execution, decision producing a decision-ready outcome, verification producing a verdict, orchestration enforcing the gates. All defer to the Director; none decides or commits. **Consistent.**

### Committing actions stay gated
The committing-action boundary is identified in reasoning ([constraint analysis](../director-reasoning-cognition/04-constraint-analysis.md)), marked in planning, checked in decision (governance alignment), confirmed in verification, and enforced in orchestration (governance control). The same marker travels the whole chain. **Consistent.**

### Read-only over prior outputs
Verification never modifies the chain it audits; orchestration never modifies responsibilities or alters information in flow; reasoning never rewrites memory. The read-only-over-what-you-consume discipline holds throughout. **Consistent.**

### Explainability and traceability
Reasoning is explainable; planning and decision are explainable and validated; verification demands the chain be accountable; orchestration maintains complete traceability. Every layer's output can be accounted for. **Consistent.**

### Consume-validated, produce-validated
A recurring pattern: each layer consumes its predecessor's *validated* output and produces a *validated* output — reasoning → approved recommendation → validated plan → decision-ready outcome → readiness verdict. **Consistent.**

## Contradictions

**None identified.** No body asserts a commitment another denies.

## Ambiguities

- **Trade-off/risk evaluation appears in multiple bodies.** Reasoning's decision principles ([7B/7C](../director-reasoning/04-decision-principles.md)), decision's risk balancing ([7E](../director-decision/04-risk-balancing.md)), and verification's risk verification ([7F](../director-verification/04-risk-verification.md)) all touch risk. On inspection these are **layered, not duplicated**: reasoning weighs it forming judgment, decision weighs it choosing among plans, verification independently re-checks it. Deliberate defense-in-depth, not contradiction.

## Duplicated concepts

- **Validation appears in planning, decision, orchestration, and verification.** Each validates a *different thing*: planning validates a plan's internal soundness, decision validates a decision outcome, verification validates the reasoning content of the whole chain, orchestration validates the *process*. Layered and distinct, not redundant.

## Conclusion

The seven bodies describe **one coherent reasoning architecture**. No contradictions. The apparent overlaps (risk, validation) are deliberate, layered defense-in-depth with distinct scopes, not duplication. **Consistency review: pass.**
