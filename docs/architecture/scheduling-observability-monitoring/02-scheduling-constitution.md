# 02 — Scheduling Constitution

## Purpose

Define Scheduling as the Runtime-bound determination of execution eligibility across declared temporal and operational constraints.

## Constitutional Identity

Scheduling is the attributable evaluation of whether an already admitted Runtime responsibility is eligible for operational consideration within a declared temporal, authority, Governance, dependency, capacity, and condition context.

Scheduling is not execution, Workflow progression, planning, admission, approval, authorization, orchestration, queueing, timer implementation, or resource deployment.

## Eligibility

Scheduling eligibility requires:

- valid Runtime admission and unexpired authority;
- applicable Governance compatibility;
- eligible Workflow and State context;
- required Event, dependency, and Memory evidence;
- declared temporal constraints;
- declared operational conditions and limitations;
- Tenant, classification, purpose, and Scope compatibility.

Eligibility indicates constitutional readiness for consideration. It does not start work.

## Authority Limits

Scheduling cannot create or renew approval, broaden Scope, choose business priority, modify Workflow, change State, emit Events as commands, allocate resources, execute, or recover.

## Rules

- **P23-SCHEDULING-001:** Scheduling must evaluate only already admitted Runtime responsibility.
- **P23-SCHEDULING-002:** Eligibility must preserve authority, Governance, Scope, Tenant, and classification.
- **P23-SCHEDULING-003:** Eligible must not be interpreted as executing, authorized anew, or guaranteed.
- **P23-SCHEDULING-004:** Missing or conflicting constraints must fail closed.
- **P23-SCHEDULING-005:** Scheduling evidence and outcome must remain attributable.
- **P23-SCHEDULING-006:** Scheduling meaning must remain independent of implementation technology.

## Enterprise Example

An approved responsibility may be eligible only during a declared business window and while required State evidence remains valid. Eligibility does not start execution.
