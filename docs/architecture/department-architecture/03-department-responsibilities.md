# 03 — Department Responsibilities

## Purpose

Department Responsibilities defines **departmental ownership and accountability** — what a department owns, what it answers for, and to whom. It is where the department model's "ownership" and "accountability line" become explicit: a department is the permanent owner of one business domain and is accountable upward for that domain's outcomes.

## Architectural role

Where the department model ([02](02-department-model.md)) defines the anatomy, this document defines what that anatomy is *responsible for*. Authority boundaries ([04](04-department-boundaries.md)) then fence the responsibility; governance ([06](06-department-governance.md)) holds it accountable. Ownership and accountability are the substance the rest of the department architecture protects.

## The model

### Ownership: a department owns its domain's outcomes
A department is the standing owner of one business domain. Every outcome within that domain has this department as its owner — there are no orphan outcomes in a department's domain, and no outcome in it is unowned ([department principles](01-department-principles.md)). Ownership is permanent, not assigned per run of work.

### Ownership is exclusive to the domain
A department owns its domain and only its domain. It does not own outcomes in another department's domain, and its domain is not co-owned by another department. Exclusive ownership is what makes accountability unambiguous ([department boundaries](04-department-boundaries.md)).

### Accountability runs upward to the Director
A department answers for its domain to the level above it and ultimately to the Director ([enterprise organizational model](../enterprise-organization/02-organizational-model.md)). Ownership without upward accountability is not permitted — a department is accountable precisely because it owns.

### Ownership does not mean doing
A department *owns* its domain's outcomes; it does not *reason about* them or *perform* the work that produces them. Reasoning stays in Director Intelligence; work stays in Execution. The department is accountable for the outcome, and routes the thinking and the doing to the domains that hold them ([department boundaries](04-department-boundaries.md)).

### Ownership and authority are paired
A department's ownership is matched by its delegated authority ([enterprise authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)): it is authorized to the extent it is accountable for its domain, and accountable to the extent it is authorized. Neither exceeds the other.

### Accountability is traceable
Who owns a domain, and the line by which they are accountable upward, can always be reconstructed and audited ([department governance](06-department-governance.md)). Accountability is a structural fact, not a runtime claim.

## Inputs

- The **department model** ([02](02-department-model.md)) — the anatomy that carries ownership.
- The **delegated authority** ([Phase 9A authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)) — the grant ownership is paired with.

## Outputs

- A **defined ownership and accountability structure** — what each department owns, exclusively, and answers for, upward — that boundaries and governance rely on.

## Boundaries

- Assigns ownership to **departments as seats, not concrete departments** — it names none.
- Defines **no work and no reasoning** — a department owns outcomes; it does not produce them.
- Describes **no runtime or mechanism** — only the structure of ownership and accountability.

## Future direction

As concrete departments are defined, each takes exclusive ownership of a real domain and a real line of accountability upward. Manager and specialist agents filling the internal seats act *within* the department's ownership; they never dilute it. More departments, same rule: exclusive domain ownership, accountable to the Director. The enterprise grows; ownership stays unambiguous.
