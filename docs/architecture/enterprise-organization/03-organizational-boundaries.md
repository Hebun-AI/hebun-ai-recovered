# 03 — Organizational Boundaries

## Purpose

The Organizational Boundaries define **what the organization does not do**. Enterprise Organization is powerful precisely because it is narrow: it structures, and nothing else. This document draws the lines that keep the organization from absorbing the responsibilities of Director Intelligence or Execution.

## Architectural role

Where the principles ([01](01-organization-principles.md)) state what the organization must obey, the boundaries state what it must refuse. Together they fence the organization into its single concern — structure — so that reasoning, work, and planning stay in their own domains.

## What the organization does not do

### It does not reason
The organization holds no reasoning, no analysis, no judgment about what should be done. When a situation needs thinking through, the organization routes it to Director Intelligence ([reasoning](../director-reasoning/README.md)); it never thinks in the reasoning domain's place.

### It does not execute work
The organization performs no tasks and produces no outcomes. When approved work must be carried out, Execution carries it out ([execution](../director-execution/README.md)); the organization only defines *who is responsible* for it and *under what authority*.

### It does not plan or re-plan
The organization produces no plans and alters none. Planning is Director Intelligence's job ([planning](../director-planning/README.md)). The organization frames where planning happens and who owns its result — never the plan itself.

### It does not decide
The organization makes no decisions on behalf of any unit. Decisions belong to Director Intelligence under Director Authority ([decision](../director-decision/README.md)). The organization defines which level *owns* a decision's outcome, not what the decision is.

### It does not originate authority
The organization creates no authority. All authority descends from the Director ([authority model](04-authority-model.md)). A unit's authority is only ever what was delegated to it — the organization records delegation; it never mints it.

### It does not define concrete participants
The organization names no department, no agent, no human role. It defines *seats*; who fills them is a later phase behind the Director gate ([organizational model](02-organizational-model.md)).

### It does not bypass Director Authority
No level, no delegation, no coordination path lets any unit act beyond the Director's authority. The Director sits atop the hierarchy and can override anything below ([authority model](04-authority-model.md)).

## Inputs

- The **organization principles** ([01](01-organization-principles.md)) and the **organizational model** ([02](02-organizational-model.md)) — the frame these boundaries protect.

## Outputs

- A **clear negative space** — the set of things the organization refuses — that keeps every other domain sovereign in its own concern.

## Boundaries

- This document **adds no capability** — it removes ambiguity about what the organization is not.
- It **describes no runtime or mechanism** — only the limits of the structural concern.

## Future direction

As the enterprise grows and adds departments, agents, and human roles, these boundaries hold unchanged. New participants gain seats and delegated authority — never the right to reason, execute, plan, decide, or originate authority from inside the organization layer. The organization stays structure, forever.
