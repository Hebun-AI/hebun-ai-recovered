# 03 — Prioritization

## Purpose

Prioritization is where decision **ranks the assessed alternatives according to organizational objectives** — ordering the candidates so the strongest for the organization rises to the top. Option Evaluation established how good each alternative is on its own terms; prioritization determines which best serves the organization *now*, given its goals, direction, and the competing claims on its attention.

## Architectural role

Prioritization sits between Option Evaluation and the final decision outcome, working alongside [Risk Balancing](04-risk-balancing.md) (a high-opportunity plan may rank lower once risk is weighed) and [Governance Alignment](05-governance-alignment.md) (a misaligned plan cannot rank at all). It carries reasoning's prioritization discipline ([decision principles](../director-reasoning/04-decision-principles.md)) to the plan level and enforces the **organization before optimization** principle ([first principles](../director-reasoning/02-first-principles.md)): ranking is by the organization's interest, never a local proxy.

## Inputs

- The **assessed alternatives** from Option Evaluation.
- The **organizational objectives and direction** — from the model, memory (including learned priorities), and the approved goal.
- The **relative importance** of competing goals where several apply.

## Outputs

- A **ranked ordering** of the alternatives, by fit to organizational objectives.
- The **rationale** for the ranking — why this order — keeping it explainable.
- Explicit note of any **near-ties or genuine tensions** between top alternatives, surfaced for the Director rather than resolved silently.

## Boundaries

- Ranking is **by the organization's objectives** — not by a single metric, venture, or convenience.
- Prioritization **orders; it does not commit** — the ranking informs the decision-ready outcome; the Director makes the choice ([decision principles](01-decision-principles.md)).
- It **produces no action** and **defines no method** — this document establishes that prioritization exists and its role, not any ranking algorithm.

## Future direction

Future decision engines may prioritize more astutely — weighing objectives more precisely, learning the organization's true priorities from memory, handling multi-objective tensions more gracefully. The discipline is fixed: rank by organizational objectives, surface genuine tensions, decide nothing the Director hasn't approved. Astuteness grows; the organization-first ordering holds.
