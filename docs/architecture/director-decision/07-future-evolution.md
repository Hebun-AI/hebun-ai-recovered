# 07 — Future Evolution

How Director Decision is expected to evolve — **at the architecture level only**. No algorithms, no prompts, no implementation, no runtime, no execution. The decision topics defined here are the stable frame; evolution deepens *how well each is performed*, never *whether the boundaries hold*.

Each direction below is future work, behind its own Director gate, following the [Capability Lifecycle](../../architecture-backlog/00-capability-lifecycle.md).

## Deeper decision-making, same architecture

Future decision engines will perform each topic more capably — more objective evaluation, sharper prioritization, finer risk balancing, more precise governance alignment, more thorough validation. But they will still follow the **same architecture**: consume validated plans, evaluate objectively, prioritize by organizational objectives, balance opportunity and risk, align with governance, validate, and produce a decision-ready outcome that never executes and never bypasses Director Authority. A decision engine that executed, or pre-favored an option, or skipped governance, would not be more advanced — it would be broken. The architecture and its boundaries are the invariant every future engine inherits.

## Toward the Execution phase

The natural next phase is **Execution** — how an approved decision's plan is carried out. This phase deliberately stops at the decision-ready outcome; execution is a separate concern, under the Director's authority, behind its own gate. The seam is clean: decision produces a validated, decision-ready outcome with its committing actions marked; the Director approves; execution runs the approved plan, honoring every mark. Decision hands off an outcome; it never runs one.

## Integration with governance engines

As the [Policy Engine](../../architecture-backlog/13-policy-engine.md) and [Permission Engine](../../architecture-backlog/14-permission-engine.md) are designed, Governance Alignment deepens — checking richer, enforceable policy and finer permission. Decision consumes stronger governance; its own boundary — governance as a hard bound, committing actions gated — does not move.

## Integration with reasoning, planning, and memory

As reasoning ([7A–7C](../director-reasoning/README.md)) and planning ([7D](../director-planning/README.md)) deepen, decision receives better recommendations and sounder plans, and decides better. As memory deepens ([Phase 6](../memory/README.md)), decision learns the organization's true objectives and risk posture. Decision consumes richer inputs; its role and boundaries stay fixed.

## The invariant across all evolution

Through every future version of Director Decision:

- Decision **consumes validated plans** — no decision over unvetted work.
- Decision **evaluates objectively, prioritizes by organizational objectives, balances risk honestly, and aligns with governance** — always.
- Decision **produces decision-ready outcomes** but **never executes** — and **never bypasses Director Authority**; committing actions stay gated.
- Capability may grow without limit; the **evaluate-then-defer-to-the-Director structure** does not change.

Director Decision can become far more capable. It cannot execute work, decide for the Director, or authorize committing actions on its own. That fixed foundation is what every future decision engine is built around.
