# 01 — Decision Principles

## Purpose

The Decision Principles are the constitution of Director Decision — the commitments every decision-ready outcome must obey. Where reasoning's first principles ([Phase 7A](../director-reasoning/02-first-principles.md)) govern forming judgment and planning's principles govern building plans, these govern producing a decision. Any decision outcome that violates one of these is not a valid Director decision.

## Architectural role

These principles constrain all the decision topics that follow (option evaluation, prioritization, risk balancing, governance alignment, validation). Every subsequent document inherits them first. They keep decision-making objective, governance-aligned, and subordinate to the Director.

## The principles

### 1. Decisions are made over validated plans
A decision outcome is produced by evaluating **validated, execution-ready plans** ([Phase 7D](../director-planning/README.md)), never raw ideas or unvetted options. Deciding over an unvalidated plan would risk committing to broken work. The plans decision evaluates have already been checked for soundness.

### 2. Evaluation is objective
Alternatives are evaluated fairly, on the evidence, with no option pre-favored. A decision architecture that tilted toward a predetermined answer would be rationalizing, not deciding. Objectivity — every alternative given an honest hearing — is the first duty of a decision.

### 3. The organization comes first
Decisions prioritize the organization's objectives, not a local metric or a single venture's gain ([organization before optimization](../director-reasoning/02-first-principles.md)). An outcome that wins locally while harming the organization is a failed decision, however attractive the part.

### 4. Opportunity and risk are both visible
A decision weighs what is to be gained against what could go wrong, with both sides explicit ([risk balancing](04-risk-balancing.md)). Presenting opportunity while hiding risk — or the reverse — corrupts the decision. Honest balance is required.

### 5. Decisions are governance-aligned
Every decision outcome conforms to the governance context — policy, permission, obligation ([governance alignment](05-governance-alignment.md)). A decision that would violate governance is invalid, no matter how good its opportunity. Governance bounds the decision as constraints bound the plan.

### 6. Decisions preserve Director Authority
The decision architecture produces a **decision-ready outcome**; the Director makes the decision. The outcome is advisory until the Director approves it, and every committing action stays gated ([Director Authority](../director-reasoning/05-director-authority.md)). Producing a decision-ready outcome never means acting on it.

### 7. Decisions are explainable and validated
Every outcome can be accounted for — which plan, why, over which alternatives, at what risk, within which governance — and is validated before it is offered ([decision validation](06-decision-validation.md)). An unexplainable or unchecked outcome is one the Director cannot own or safely approve.

## Inputs

- The **validated plan(s)** from planning, and the **approved goal and context** they serve.
- The **governance context** the decision must align with.

## Outputs

- A **principled frame** every decision activity operates within — the standard each outcome is held to.

## Boundaries

- These principles **define no method** — they state what decision outcomes must obey, not how deciding is performed.
- They **authorize no action** — a principled outcome is still only decision-ready, awaiting the Director's approval to execute.

## Future direction

Future decision engines may satisfy these principles more thoroughly — evaluating more objectively, aligning governance more precisely, explaining more clearly. The principles are fixed: validated plans, objective evaluation, organization first, honest risk, governance-aligned, authority-preserving, explainable and validated. Capability grows; the constitution holds.
