# 08 — Escalation Principles

## Purpose

Define escalation as attributable communication of responsibility and review need without automatic authority transfer.

## Escalation Identity

Escalation identifies a matter that cannot remain constitutionally resolved within the current Runtime responsibility and declares the appropriate accountable review boundary.

Escalation is not a decision, approval, authority transfer, message protocol, queue, workflow, control execution, recovery, or automation.

## Escalation Content

An escalation preserves subject, evidence, provenance, policy, authority, failure classification, current Control, Runtime condition, affected Scope, uncertainty, attempted boundaries, eligible recipient, urgency context, and required decision or review.

## Rules

- **P24-ESCALATION-001:** Escalation communicates responsibility but never transfers constitutional authority automatically.
- **P24-ESCALATION-002:** Every escalation must identify an existing accountable recipient boundary.
- **P24-ESCALATION-003:** Urgency must not create authority.
- **P24-ESCALATION-004:** Escalation must preserve evidence, uncertainty, and dissent.
- **P24-ESCALATION-005:** Escalation acknowledgment must not imply approval or resolution.
- **P24-ESCALATION-006:** Escalation semantics must not define messaging or workflow implementation.

## Enterprise Example

Runtime may expose a recovery-authority gap to the Director boundary. Exposure does not transfer Director authority to Runtime.
