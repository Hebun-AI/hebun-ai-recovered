# 12 — Operational Resilience

## Purpose

Define the constitutional ability to preserve safe, accountable continuity through deviation, failure, degradation, escalation, and authorized recovery.

## Constitutional Identity

Operational Resilience is the bounded capacity of the Runtime Platform to preserve or restore valid operational responsibility while maintaining constitutional authority, safety, traceability, accountability, policy, evidence, and human control.

Operational Resilience is not High Availability, uptime, redundancy, self-healing, automated recovery, infrastructure architecture, disaster recovery implementation, or permission to continue at any cost.

## Resilience Dimensions

- constitutional integrity;
- safe continuity;
- evidence preservation;
- bounded Control;
- failure visibility;
- escalation continuity;
- Human Override availability;
- Safe Degradation;
- authorized recovery;
- accountable closure and learning evidence.

## Continuity Boundary

Operational continuity never overrides constitutional compliance. When both cannot be preserved, Runtime must favor fail-closed, Human Override, or Safe Degradation according to valid authority and policy.

## Rules

- **P24-RESILIENCE-001:** Operational Resilience must preserve safety, traceability, and accountability.
- **P24-RESILIENCE-002:** Operational continuity never overrides constitutional compliance.
- **P24-RESILIENCE-003:** Resilience must not create Runtime, recovery, or exception authority.
- **P24-RESILIENCE-004:** Operational Resilience ≠ High Availability.
- **P24-RESILIENCE-005:** Evidence and limitations must remain visible across continuity and recovery.
- **P24-RESILIENCE-006:** Operational Resilience must remain implementation-independent.

## Enterprise Example

A Runtime may degrade safely rather than remain fully available when policy evidence is invalid. Availability is subordinate to constitutional integrity.
