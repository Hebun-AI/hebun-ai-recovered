# 03 — Ownership Transfer

## Purpose

Ownership Transfer defines **how ownership moves between units, safely** — the explicit rules by which a department's domain outcome or a specialist's capability outcome passes from one owner to another. It exists so that ownership can move when the enterprise needs it to, without ever becoming ambiguous, implicit, or unaccountable.

## Architectural role

Where the interaction model ([02](02-organizational-interaction-model.md)) defines how units meet, ownership transfer defines one specific, high-stakes interaction: a change of owner. It protects the ownership that departments ([9B](../department-architecture/03-department-responsibilities.md)) and specialists ([9D](../specialist-architecture/03-specialist-responsibilities.md)) hold, and it is bounded by the same authority and governance the rest of collaboration obeys.

## The model

### Ownership always has exactly one owner
Before, during, and after any transfer, an outcome has exactly one owner ([collaboration principles](01-collaboration-principles.md)). There is no moment of shared, dual, or absent ownership. Transfer is a clean handoff, not a period of ambiguity.

### Transfer is explicit and governed
Ownership moves only through an explicit transfer, never implicitly and never as a side effect of collaborating ([collaboration principles](01-collaboration-principles.md)). Every transfer is a deliberate, recorded act under the enterprise governance regime ([collaboration governance](06-collaboration-governance.md)).

### Transfer is authorized
A transfer happens only within the delegated authority of the units and levels involved. A specialist cannot transfer its capability ownership on its own authority beyond its grant; where a transfer exceeds the participants' authority, it escalates ([escalation model](05-escalation-model.md)) — ultimately to the Director for committing or high-stakes transfers ([enterprise authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)).

### Transfer respects the hierarchy
Ownership moves along authorized paths ([organizational interaction model](02-organizational-interaction-model.md)). A cross-department transfer flows through the managers of both departments ([cross-department coordination](04-cross-department-coordination.md)); it does not pass directly between units in a way that bypasses their managers' authority.

### Transfer carries accountability with ownership
When ownership moves, accountability for that outcome moves with it — the new owner answers for it upward through its own line ([manager principles](../manager-architecture/01-manager-principles.md), [specialist principles](../specialist-architecture/01-specialist-principles.md)). Ownership and accountability never separate: you cannot transfer the outcome while leaving the accountability behind.

### Transfer preserves higher ownership
A specialist-to-specialist transfer within a department leaves the department's domain ownership intact; a department-to-department transfer moves domain-scoped ownership under the enterprise level's authority ([department responsibilities](../department-architecture/03-department-responsibilities.md)). A transfer never violates the nesting of capability-within-domain-within-enterprise.

### Transfer is fully traceable
Every transfer — what moved, from whom, to whom, under whose authority, when — can be reconstructed and audited ([collaboration governance](06-collaboration-governance.md)). Ownership history is never lost in a handoff.

## Inputs

- The **ownership structures** of departments and specialists ([9B](../department-architecture/03-department-responsibilities.md), [9D](../specialist-architecture/03-specialist-responsibilities.md)) — what can be transferred.
- The **authority model** ([9A](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)) — what authorizes a transfer.

## Outputs

- A **defined transfer rule set** — how ownership moves, cleanly, authorized, with accountability, traceably — that keeps ownership unambiguous across collaboration.

## Boundaries

- Transfers ownership between **seats, not concrete units** — it names none.
- Defines **no work, reasoning, or plan** — it moves ownership, not tasks.
- Manufactures **no authority** — transfers stay within delegated grants and escalate beyond them.
- Describes **no runtime or mechanism** — only the structure of transfer.

## Future direction

As human and AI participants own capabilities and domains, ownership transfers among them under these same rules: one owner always, explicit and governed, authorized, through the hierarchy, accountability carried, traceable. More owners, same clean handoff. The enterprise grows; ownership stays unambiguous through every transfer.
