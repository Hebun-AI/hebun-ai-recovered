# 01 — Collaboration Principles

## Purpose

The Collaboration Principles are the constitution of Cross-Organization Collaboration — the commitments any cross-unit collaboration must obey. Collaboration is where units meet; these principles are what keep that meeting from dissolving ownership, blurring accountability, or crossing authority. Any collaboration that violates one of these is not doing Cross-Organization Collaboration.

## Architectural role

These principles constrain all the collaboration topics that follow (interaction model, ownership transfer, cross-department coordination, escalation, governance). Every subsequent document inherits them first. They keep collaboration structural, ownership-preserving, authority-respecting, governed, and traceable.

## The principles

### 1. Collaboration is structural, not operational
Collaboration arranges *which units relate, under what authority, with ownership preserved*. It performs no reasoning ([Director Intelligence](../director-reasoning/README.md)), no work ([Execution](../director-execution/README.md)), and no orchestration of running work ([execution orchestration](../execution-orchestration/README.md)). It coordinates relationships, never tasks.

### 2. Ownership is preserved through collaboration
Every unit keeps its ownership when it collaborates: departments keep domain ownership ([department responsibilities](../department-architecture/03-department-responsibilities.md)), specialists keep capability ownership ([specialist responsibilities](../specialist-architecture/03-specialist-responsibilities.md)). Collaboration aligns owners; it never dissolves ownership into a committee.

### 3. Ownership transfers only by explicit, governed rule
Ownership may move between units, but only through the explicit ownership-transfer rules ([ownership transfer](03-ownership-transfer.md)) — never implicitly, never as a side effect of collaborating. An outcome always has exactly one owner before, during, and after any transfer.

### 4. Accountability is never shed by collaborating
A manager stays accountable for its department ([manager principles](../manager-architecture/01-manager-principles.md)) and a specialist stays accountable to its manager ([specialist principles](../specialist-architecture/01-specialist-principles.md)), no matter how they collaborate. Collaboration extends reach; it never sheds responsibility.

### 5. Collaboration flows through organizational authority
Units collaborate through the authority structure, never around it ([enterprise authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)). Cross-department specialist collaboration flows through the managers; nothing opens a side-channel that bypasses a manager's authority or a unit's delegation.

### 6. Beyond-authority matters escalate, never overreach
When a collaboration exceeds the delegated authority of the units involved, it escalates upward — ultimately toward the Director ([escalation model](05-escalation-model.md)) — rather than any unit acting beyond its grant. Escalation, not overreach, is the response to a limit.

### 7. Collaboration preserves Director Authority
No collaboration, transfer, or coordination path lets any unit act beyond the Director's authority. Committing actions still rest on Director approval ([enterprise authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)). The Director sits above every collaboration.

### 8. Collaboration is governed and traceable
Every collaboration falls under the enterprise-wide governance regime, and its interactions, transfers, coordinations, and escalations can be reconstructed and audited ([collaboration governance](06-collaboration-governance.md)). No collaboration is outside governance; none is exempt from traceability.

## Inputs

- The **Enterprise, Department, Manager, and Specialist architectures** ([9A](../enterprise-organization/README.md)–[9D](../specialist-architecture/README.md)) — the units and authority collaboration operates across.

## Outputs

- A **principled frame** every collaboration topic operates within — the standard any cross-unit collaboration is held to.

## Boundaries

- These principles **define no concrete unit, workflow, or task** — they state what collaboration must obey, not what runs.
- They **describe no runtime or mechanism** — collaboration machinery is a later phase behind the Director gate.

## Future direction

Future collaborations — among human and AI participants across many departments — will still obey these principles: structural not operational, ownership preserved, transfer only by rule, accountability never shed, through authority not around it, escalate at the limit, Director-topped, governed and traceable. Participants grow; the constitution holds.
