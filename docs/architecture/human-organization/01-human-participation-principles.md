# 01 — Human Participation Principles

## Purpose

The Human Participation Principles are the constitution of Human Organization — the commitments that govern how humans participate in the enterprise. Their whole purpose is to state, precisely, that humans participate *through the existing structure*, not beside it. Any arrangement that treats humans as an exception, or forks the organization in two, is not doing Human Organization.

## Architectural role

These principles constrain all the human-organization topics that follow (role model, authority, human–AI collaboration, accountability, governance). Every subsequent document inherits them first. They keep human participation first-class, seat-bound, single-structured, authority-preserving, governed, and traceable.

## The principles

### 1. Humans are participants, not exceptions
Humans occupy organizational seats and participate through the same structure as any other occupant. There is no special human path, no exemption, no carve-out. A human in a seat is bound by the seat's rules ([human role model](02-human-role-model.md)).

### 2. There is one organization, not two
Human participation creates no separate human organization running alongside the AI one. Humans and AI participants inhabit a single structure with a single line of accountability to the Director ([README](README.md)). Forking the organization is forbidden.

### 3. The seat defines the rules, not the occupant
A seat's authority, ownership, accountability, and governance are properties of the seat ([enterprise organizational model](../enterprise-organization/02-organizational-model.md)). A human occupant inherits exactly those properties — the architecture governs the seat, never the nature of who fills it.

### 4. Humans and AI participants are equal as occupants
Neither humans nor AI participants are the default; neither is the exception. Both are held to the identical seat rules ([human–AI collaboration](04-human-ai-collaboration.md)). They differ in nature and capability; they are equal in how the seat binds them.

### 5. A human occupant's authority is delegated and revocable
A human in a seat holds only the authority the seat's delegation grants, and that grant is always revocable ([human authority](03-human-authority.md), [enterprise authority model](../enterprise-organization/04-authority-model.md)). No human occupant originates authority or sits above the Director.

### 6. Human participation preserves the hierarchy
Humans occupy seats *within* the hierarchy and respect its accountability and authority lines ([enterprise organizational model](../enterprise-organization/02-organizational-model.md)). A human manager governs a department; a human specialist reports to its manager. Being human changes none of the hierarchy's relationships.

### 7. Humans fall under the one governance regime
Every human occupant is inside the enterprise-wide governance regime, with no exemption ([human governance](06-human-governance.md)). Human participation redefines no governance; it inherits the existing regime unchanged.

### 8. Human participation is traceable
A human occupant's seat, authority, ownership, accountability, and collaborations can be reconstructed and audited exactly as any occupant's ([human governance](06-human-governance.md)). Traceability does not weaken because the occupant is human.

### 9. This layer defines participation, not reasoning or work
Human Organization defines how humans *fit* the structure. It performs no reasoning ([Director Intelligence](../director-reasoning/README.md)) and no work ([Execution](../director-execution/README.md)), and it defines no authentication, permission, UI, or workflow ([README](README.md)). It is structural only.

## Inputs

- The **Enterprise, Department, Manager, Specialist, and Collaboration architectures** ([9A](../enterprise-organization/README.md)–[9E](../cross-organization-collaboration/README.md)) — the single structure humans participate in.

## Outputs

- A **principled frame** for human participation — the standard by which humans occupy seats, held identical to any occupant.

## Boundaries

- These principles **define no concrete human role or job title** — they state how humans participate, not who they are.
- They **describe no runtime, authentication, permission, or UI** — those are out of scope, behind the Director gate.

## Future direction

Future hybrid organizations — any mix of human and AI occupants across many departments — will still obey these principles: participants not exceptions, one organization, seat defines the rules, humans and AI equal as occupants, delegated authority, hierarchy preserved, one governance, traceable. The mix changes; the constitution holds.
