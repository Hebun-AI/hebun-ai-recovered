# 10 — Memory Retention Principles

## Purpose

Define why and for how long Enterprise Memory remains constitutionally retained without choosing a storage or deletion implementation.

## Retention Basis

Retention is governed by declared organizational value, evidentiary need, legal and regulatory obligation, contractual duty, privacy constraint, historical accountability, audit need, lifecycle state, classification, Tenant, and authorized purpose.

Retention is not indefinite by default. Convenience, technical capacity, analytical possibility, or future speculation is not sufficient basis.

## Disposition

At the end of a retention period, an authorized disposition determines continued retention, restriction, archival treatment, or eligible removal. The disposition preserves required evidence that the decision occurred without retaining prohibited content beyond authority.

## Legal and Governance Holds

Applicable holds suspend ordinary disposition for a declared reason and Scope. A hold is not permanent ownership, unrestricted consumption, or proof of truth.

## Rules

- **P20-RETENTION-001:** Every Memory Item must have an explicit retention basis and accountable authority.
- **P20-RETENTION-002:** Retention must remain classification-, Tenant-, purpose-, and lifecycle-aware.
- **P20-RETENTION-003:** Technical capacity must not justify indefinite retention.
- **P20-RETENTION-004:** A hold must be attributable, bounded, reviewable, and historically visible.
- **P20-RETENTION-005:** Disposition must preserve mandatory provenance and audit evidence.
- **P20-RETENTION-006:** Retention must not be used to bypass privacy, consent, contractual, or organizational boundaries.

## Enterprise Example

Candidate information is retained only for the declared HR purpose and period unless a valid hold applies. Potential future analytical value cannot silently extend it.
