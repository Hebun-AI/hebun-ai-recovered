# 16 — Constitutional Conflict Handling

## Purpose

Define how Phase 20 identifies, classifies, preserves, and escalates conflicts without deciding the underlying matter or introducing operational resolution behavior.

## Constitutional Identity

A constitutional conflict is an attributable incompatibility among claims, Memory versions, provenance, ownership, classification, Tenant, Scope, purpose, authority, lifecycle, trust qualification, interpretation, or Governance constraints within an otherwise declared coordination context.

Conflict handling means constitutional interpretation, boundary preservation, and escalation-eligibility determination. It is not negotiation, voting, ranking, arbitration, consensus production, remediation, workflow, execution, or Director decision.

## Conflict Types

- **Evidence conflict:** admitted items support incompatible claims.
- **Version conflict:** participants rely on incompatible or superseded versions.
- **Provenance conflict:** lineage or attribution is missing, incompatible, or disputed.
- **Ownership conflict:** accountable stewardship or source authority is inconsistent.
- **Classification conflict:** handling boundaries are incompatible or ambiguous.
- **Tenant conflict:** information would cross an unauthorized Tenant boundary.
- **Scope or purpose conflict:** intended use exceeds admitted bounds.
- **Authority conflict:** participation or interpretation claims exceed constitutional authority.
- **Lifecycle conflict:** active, restricted, deprecated, archived, or disposition contexts are incompatible.
- **Governance conflict:** applicable policies, constraints, exceptions, or review duties are incompatible.
- **Interpretation conflict:** bounded participants reach different attributable interpretations.

## Classification

Each conflict classification preserves affected participants, Memory identities and versions, provenance, boundary dimensions, impact, uncertainty, known constraints, and escalation basis. Classification describes the conflict; it does not determine which claim is correct.

## Conflict Resolution Boundary

Phase 20 may:

- preserve every attributable position;
- identify the constitutional boundary at issue;
- state whether continued coordination remains eligible;
- require fail-closed behavior;
- expose missing authority or information;
- determine whether Governance, human, or Director escalation is eligible.

Phase 20 may not select a winning claim, amend Memory, grant an exception, approve a position, authorize work, or execute a resolution.

## Escalation Principles

Escalation preserves the full conflict record and routes nothing operationally. It identifies the constitutionally appropriate review boundary and the information required for that boundary. Escalation eligibility is not escalation execution, approval, or decision.

## Rules

- **P20-CONFLICT-001:** Every material conflict must remain visible, attributable, and classification-specific.
- **P20-CONFLICT-002:** Conflict classification must not modify evidence, Memory, provenance, or participant interpretation.
- **P20-CONFLICT-003:** Tenant, classification, authority, or Governance incompatibility must fail closed.
- **P20-CONFLICT-004:** Unresolved conflict must not be hidden through aggregation, confidence, majority, or consensus.
- **P20-CONFLICT-005:** Continued coordination eligibility must remain distinct from correctness or decision authority.
- **P20-CONFLICT-006:** Escalation eligibility must identify the appropriate boundary without invoking a workflow, message, tool, or Runtime.
- **P20-CONFLICT-007:** Governance or Director review must not be inferred as approval.
- **P20-CONFLICT-008:** Only the constitutionally authorized authority may decide or approve the underlying matter.

## Enterprise Example

Sales and Finance Intelligence may cite different admitted revenue versions. Phase 20 identifies a version conflict, preserves both interpretations, and marks the matter eligible for the appropriate review. It does not choose a forecast or authorize a commercial decision.
