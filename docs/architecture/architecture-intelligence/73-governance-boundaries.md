# 73 — Governance Intelligence Boundaries

## Definition

The **Governance Intelligence Boundary** separates analytical governance review from authority, permission, policy creation, enforcement, management, Director decision, execution, and Runtime control.

Governance Intelligence can explain whether a response appears aligned with governing constraints. It cannot become the institution that creates or enforces those constraints.

## Permitted Responsibilities

Governance Intelligence may:

- assess compliance against approved, applicable constraints;
- recommend an escalation level;
- validate represented authority and decision rights;
- check canonical architecture boundaries;
- analyze policy alignment;
- identify missing governance evidence;
- expose authority, policy, architecture, and boundary conflicts;
- attach conditions and prohibited uses to a safe response;
- construct a Director review or decision package;
- refuse to present an unsafe response as compliant.

## Prohibited Responsibilities

Governance Intelligence must not:

- grant approval, authorization, permission, exception, or authority;
- create, amend, repeal, waive, or enforce policy;
- modify, supersede, deprecate, approve, or archive canonical architecture;
- initiate, schedule, direct, pause, or cancel execution;
- modify Runtime state or infrastructure;
- make, simulate, predict, or impersonate a Director decision;
- perform organizational management;
- create a Capability or transfer Capability ownership;
- silently repair evidence, policy, or architecture;
- use confidence as permission or authority;
- perform authentication or identity proofing.

## Boundary Matrix

| Adjacent Domain | Governance Intelligence May | Governance Intelligence Must Not |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority** | Validate how authority is represented and whether it applies | Create, exercise, transfer, or amplify authority |
| **Permission** | State that permission evidence is required or unresolved | Decide whether an actor is permitted |
| **Policy** | Analyze alignment with approved policy statements | Create, evaluate executable rules, or enforce policy |
| **Director** | Prepare safe evidence, findings, alternatives, and decision questions | Replace or pre-empt Director judgment |
| **Reasoning** | Review a validated Reasoning Result and Trace | Re-perform reasoning or alter its result silently |
| **Execution** | Identify that execution authorization is absent or required | Start, stop, coordinate, or authorize execution |
| **Runtime** | Consider classified Runtime observations as evidence | Control Runtime or let observations redefine architecture |
| **Canonical Architecture** | Validate conformance and identify impact or conflict | Write back, correct, or approve a change |
| **Management** | Explain governance obligations affecting management | Assign work, manage people, or operate the enterprise |

## Required Distinctions

- **Governance ≠ Execution**
- **Governance ≠ Decision**
- **Governance ≠ Mutation**
- **Governance ≠ Director**
- **Governance ≠ Authority**
- **Governance ≠ Permission**
- **Governance ≠ Policy Enforcement Engine**
- **Governance ≠ Management**
- **Validation ≠ Authorization**
- **Recommendation ≠ Approval**

## Safe Response Boundary

An Automatic Safe Response may narrow, qualify, refuse, or escalate the presentation of intelligence. It cannot change the underlying canonical source, policy, evidence, Reasoning Result, or Runtime condition.

Safe response behavior must remain:

- transparent about what was withheld or qualified;
- traceable to governance evidence;
- proportionate to the identified risk;
- non-committing;
- reversible through renewed review;
- subordinate to Director authority.

## Enterprise Example

Governance Intelligence may determine that a proposed Agent binding lacks permission evidence and requires Director review. It cannot infer permission from the Agent's Capability eligibility, grant the binding, or trigger execution.

## Future Evolution

Future policy and permission engines may enforce decisions in Runtime. They must consume approved governance and authority contracts without turning Governance Intelligence into an enforcement or execution service.
