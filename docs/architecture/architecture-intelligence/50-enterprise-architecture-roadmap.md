# 50 — Enterprise Master Roadmap

## Purpose

The Enterprise Master Roadmap is Hebun AI's long-term canonical architecture direction map. It gives the Director one governed view of:

- the complete enterprise architecture evolution;
- the canonical Phase 1–30 program;
- the problem and objective assigned to each phase;
- dependencies between architecture layers;
- current completion status;
- the controlled boundary for future extensions.

This document is not technical implementation, delivery scheduling, a Runtime plan, or permission to begin a future phase. It is an architecture direction map governed by the Director.

## Vision

Hebun AI's long-term objective is to become a governed **Enterprise AI Operating System** capable of:

- understanding its own canonical architecture;
- reasoning with evidence while preserving authority;
- coordinating an enterprise agent ecosystem;
- operating through a reliable, observable, policy-governed Runtime;
- integrating securely with enterprise systems;
- evolving continuously without losing identity, provenance, accountability, or Director control.

The target is not uncontrolled autonomy. It is a Director-governed enterprise system in which intelligence, agents, Runtime, and platform evolution remain bounded by canonical architecture.

## Architectural Evolution

Hebun grows through five cumulative architecture layers:

```text
Foundation
    ↓
Intelligence
    ↓
Multi-Agent
    ↓
Runtime
    ↓
Enterprise Operating System
```

Each layer depends on the approved outputs and invariants of the preceding layers:

- **Foundation** defines canonical enterprise meaning, governance, memory, organization, capabilities, and architecture knowledge.
- **Intelligence** makes architecture knowledge analyzable, explainable, queryable, and governance-aware.
- **Multi-Agent** introduces a coordinated enterprise participant ecosystem under established authority.
- **Runtime** realizes approved work with state, events, scheduling, observation, and operational governance.
- **Enterprise Operating System** unifies security, policy, deployment, resilience, integration, and continuous platform evolution.

No layer may be skipped. A later layer may extend an earlier layer but must not silently replace its identity, authority, boundaries, or historical decisions.

### Architecture Intelligence Completion Amendment

**Status: DIRECTOR-GOVERNED ROADMAP AMENDMENT**

#### Purpose

The Architecture Intelligence Completion Amendment closes the architectural continuity gap between the immutable outputs of the Architecture Intelligence Platform and the existing Director Decision Architecture. It establishes the protected, non-numbered bridge:

```text
Reasoning Output Package
        +
Governance Outcome Package
        ↓
Decision Support Package
        ↓
Phase 7E — Director Decision Architecture
```

This amendment is not a numbered phase, an execution roadmap item, a new authority domain, or permission to create implementation. It does not change the identity, order, or completion record of any canonical phase.

#### Architectural Responsibility

The amendment is responsible only for defining the roadmap-level obligation that immutable Reasoning and Governance outputs must have a bounded, traceable, explainable, and non-authoritative Decision Support Package as their downstream bridge to Phase 7E.

The Decision Support Package may preserve alternatives, evaluation criteria, evidence references, governance constraints, conflicts, uncertainty, limitations, and provenance. It must not modify either input package, select on behalf of the Director, create a recommendation, grant approval, authorize work, initiate execution, or become canonical truth.

The following separations are mandatory:

- Reasoning ≠ Decision
- Governance ≠ Decision
- Decision Support ≠ Director Authority
- Decision Support ≠ Recommendation
- Decision Support ≠ Approval
- Decision Support ≠ Execution
- Director Decision = Final Authority

#### Dependencies and Relationships

- **Phase 7 — Director Intelligence:** Phase 7E remains the canonical Director Decision Architecture. The amendment supplies bounded decision support to that architecture and neither replaces it nor transfers its authority.
- **Phase 14 — Architecture Reasoning Engine:** The immutable Reasoning Output Package is one required input. Its evidence, assumptions, inferences, conflicts, confidence, limitations, and provenance remain unchanged.
- **Phase 15 — Architecture Query Intelligence:** Query Intelligence remains upstream preparation for reasoning. It does not produce, route, approve, or interpret a Decision Support Package.
- **Phase 16 — Governance Intelligence:** The immutable Governance Outcome Package is one required input. Eligibility findings, constraints, review requirements, authority limitations, and provenance remain unchanged.
- **Phase 17 — Director & Architecture Agents:** Phase 17 retains its canonical identity. Any later agent participation that presents decision support must consume the governed bridge without deciding, approving, authorizing, or executing on behalf of the Director.

#### Completion Criteria

The amendment is complete only when Director review confirms that:

1. both input packages remain immutable and traceable;
2. the Decision Support Package has an explicit, non-authoritative boundary;
3. Phase 7E remains the sole canonical decision destination;
4. Director approval cannot be inferred from package content, confidence, governance eligibility, agent activity, or system state;
5. recommendation, approval, execution, workflow, Runtime, and agent orchestration remain outside the amendment;
6. Phase 17 entry preserves the amendment's authority and package boundaries;
7. backward compatibility with completed Phases 7 and 12–16 is validated.

#### Director Authority Preservation

The amendment prepares structured material for Director judgment; it does not make the judgment. Phase 7E evaluates the decision-ready material under its existing architecture, and the Director retains final authority to accept, reject, defer, or override. No Decision Support Package field, status, score, ranking, confidence level, or governance outcome may be interpreted as Director approval.

#### Runtime and Execution Exclusion

The Decision Support Package is not a command, execution plan, authorization token, workflow trigger, agent assignment, tool invocation, or Runtime admission artifact. Runtime and execution layers must reject it as a direct basis for action. Only work that has crossed the existing Director approval, verification, and execution-admission boundaries may be realized.

#### Backward Compatibility

This amendment:

- preserves Phase 1–30 numbering, titles, identities, ordering, and dependencies;
- preserves Phase 7E as the canonical Director Decision Architecture;
- preserves all completed Phase 12–16 documents and package contracts;
- adds no Recommendation Intelligence, Decision Intelligence authority, Multi-Agent architecture, Runtime architecture, workflow, execution, or implementation;
- introduces no retroactive change to completed architecture evidence;
- makes Phase 17 continuity explicit without changing Phase 17 Scope.

## Phase Dependencies

```text
Phase 1–11: Foundation
        ↓ required canonical baseline
Phase 12–16: Intelligence
        ↓ required governed understanding
Phase 17–20: Multi-Agent
        ↓ required coordinated participants
Phase 21–24: Runtime
        ↓ required operational realization
Phase 25–30: Enterprise Operating System
```

Dependency rules:

1. Intelligence must not precede a trustworthy canonical Foundation.
2. Multi-Agent architecture must not precede governed Intelligence and existing authority models.
3. Runtime must not precede bounded participants, execution contracts, and governance.
4. Enterprise Operating System architecture must not precede a governed Runtime foundation.
5. A later phase may begin only through an explicit Director gate.
6. Progress in a later group must not rewrite the completion record of an earlier group.

## Completion Model

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PLANNED** | Canonical roadmap intent exists, but the phase's detailed architecture has not begun. |
| **IN PROGRESS** | The phase or phase group is actively producing architecture and has not reached review closure. |
| **DIRECTOR REVIEW** | Required artifacts and validation are complete and await Director approval or closure decision. |
| **COMPLETE** | The phase is approved and closed within its declared architectural Scope. |
| **ARCHIVED** | The phase record is retained for history and audit but is no longer active; archival does not erase provenance. |

Status applies to roadmap governance. It is not Runtime state, implementation progress telemetry, or implicit approval.

## Future Phases

### Phase 31+ — Future Extension Phases

Phase 31 and later may extend Hebun into new enterprise domains, capabilities, integration models, or platform evolution areas.

The Phase 1–30 program is the **Canonical Foundation Roadmap**. Future phases:

- may extend the roadmap;
- must preserve Phase 1–30 identity, ordering, history, and dependencies;
- must not reuse existing phase numbers;
- must not retroactively change completion evidence;
- require explicit Scope, dependency analysis, backward-compatibility review, and Director approval.

Phase 31+ does not imply pre-approval of any particular extension.

## Roadmap Design Rules

1. **ROADMAP-001 — Roadmap Stability:** Phase 1–30 identity, ordering, grouping, and historical status must remain stable.
2. **ROADMAP-002 — Phase Dependency:** Every phase must preserve and satisfy applicable dependencies on prior phases.
3. **ROADMAP-003 — Layer Order:** Foundation, Intelligence, Multi-Agent, Runtime, and Enterprise Operating System layers must not be reordered or skipped.
4. **ROADMAP-004 — Architecture Evolution:** Later phases may extend earlier architecture but must not silently replace canonical meaning or authority.
5. **ROADMAP-005 — Backward Compatibility:** Every future phase must assess compatibility with applicable completed architecture and invariants.
6. **ROADMAP-006 — Director Governance:** Phase start, status transition, Scope change, completion, archival, and extension require Director governance.
7. **ROADMAP-007 — Canonical Roadmap Protection:** Phase 1–30 is the protected Canonical Foundation Roadmap and must not be altered by an unapproved downstream phase.
8. **ROADMAP-008 — Extension Policy:** Phase 31+ may add new governed phases only after dependency, identity, numbering, and compatibility validation.
9. **ROADMAP-009 — Unique Phase Number:** A phase number must identify exactly one canonical roadmap phase.
10. **ROADMAP-010 — Duplicate Prevention:** Existing phase numbers must not be reused, aliased, or silently renumbered.
11. **ROADMAP-011 — Status Integrity:** Roadmap status must be evidence-based and must not be inferred from implementation activity or calendar time.
12. **ROADMAP-012 — Non-implementation Boundary:** This roadmap must not be interpreted as a technology selection, implementation authorization, delivery commitment, or execution plan.

## Director View

The Director uses this roadmap to evaluate:

- current architectural position;
- completed foundations;
- active architecture group;
- next eligible phase;
- unresolved dependency or governance gates;
- whether a proposal extends or violates the canonical roadmap.

The roadmap informs Director judgment. It does not automate phase transitions or approval.
