# Heby Interaction Model

## Document Status

**Document type:** Living Product Vision  
**Status:** FUTURE PRODUCT VISION — NOT OPEN

This document records the long-term interaction philosophy between Enterprise Directors, enterprise users, and Heby. It is the permanent Product Vision home for future Heby interaction ideas, conversation concepts, and command concepts.

This document does not authorize implementation. It defines no architecture, Runtime, APIs, interface components, databases, technologies, constitutional ownership, roadmap commitments, Programs, or Phases.

## Interaction Vision

Heby should make enterprise intelligence feel understandable, conversational, and available at the moment of need.

People should be able to express intent in their own language, explore enterprise context through dialogue, ask for explanations and evidence, and retain accountable control over consequential decisions. Interaction should reduce the distance between a person, a question, and the enterprise knowledge required to understand it.

Heby is an advisor and interaction partner. It does not replace human accountability, organizational authority, or enterprise judgment.

## Natural Language First

Natural language is the primary interaction philosophy for Heby.

Directors and enterprise users should be able to ask questions, describe goals, explore uncertainty, request summaries, and refine ideas without first learning a command language. Heby should understand conversational intent within the person's authorized enterprise context and respond at an appropriate level of depth.

Natural Language First means:

- People begin with the language they already use.
- Follow-up questions preserve meaningful conversational context.
- Heby helps clarify ambiguous intent rather than silently assuming it.
- Enterprise terminology and organizational meaning remain understandable.
- Structured commands remain optional accelerators, not prerequisites.

The goal is not unrestricted automation. The goal is more natural access to enterprise understanding.

## Slash Commands

Slash Commands are a future conceptual shorthand for frequent, explicit interaction intents.

They may help a person quickly request a known form of assistance while preserving the ability to continue in natural language. A slash command should communicate intent clearly and predictably without turning Heby into a technical console.

Conceptual examples may include:

- `/why` — Ask why Heby reached or presented a conclusion.
- `/evidence` — Ask for the evidence supporting the current response.
- `/sources` — Ask which sources informed the response.
- `/summarize` — Ask for a concise synthesis of current context.
- `/compare` — Ask for a conceptual comparison of relevant alternatives.
- `/review` — Ask to prepare an item for human review.
- `/approve` — Express an approval-related intent subject to applicable authority and safeguards.

These examples define product intent only. They do not define syntax contracts, execution semantics, permissions, workflows, or technical behavior.

## Command Palette

The Command Palette is a future conceptual discovery layer for Heby interactions.

It may help people discover relevant questions, commands, review actions, conversation modes, and explainability options without requiring memorization. Its purpose is orientation and confidence, not feature density.

A future Command Palette should conceptually:

- Reveal what Heby can help with in the current context.
- Distinguish advisory, review, and approval-related intents.
- Make consequences and authority boundaries understandable.
- Prioritize relevant options over exhaustive lists.
- Preserve natural language as the primary path.

No interface design, component, keyboard behavior, or implementation is defined here.

## Context Awareness

Heby should understand interaction in relation to the enterprise context available to the person.

Conceptual context may include:

- The person's enterprise role and responsibilities
- The current Enterprise Domain
- The subject currently under discussion
- Relevant decisions, knowledge, documents, and timeline events
- The current conversation and its unresolved questions
- The distinction between Director and standard enterprise experiences

Context Awareness should improve relevance while remaining bounded, explainable, and respectful of enterprise authority. Heby should not imply knowledge it does not possess or access a context merely because it might be useful.

People should be able to understand which context matters to a response and ask Heby to narrow, broaden, or clarify that context conceptually.

## Smart Suggestions

Smart Suggestions are future advisory prompts that help people identify useful next questions or review paths.

Suggestions may reflect the current conversation, enterprise signals, pending decisions, knowledge changes, or uncertainty in Heby's response. They should encourage thoughtful interaction rather than passive acceptance.

Good suggestions should be:

- Contextual
- Concise
- Explainable
- Non-authoritative
- Easy to ignore
- Relevant to the person's role
- Clear about whether they invite inquiry, review, or approval

Suggestions never create authority and should not imply that a recommended action has already been approved.

## Explainability

Explainability should be available as a natural continuation of conversation rather than a separate specialist experience.

People should be able to ask:

- Why did Heby present this conclusion?
- What evidence supports it?
- Which sources informed it?
- Which Enterprise Domains participated?
- What assumptions or uncertainties remain?
- What changed since the previous understanding?
- Which decisions or timeline events are related?

Conceptual explainability commands may include `/why`, `/evidence`, `/sources`, and future equivalents. These commands are conversational entry points, not guarantees that every form of internal reasoning can or should be exposed.

Heby should distinguish among evidence, source provenance, interpretation, uncertainty, recommendation, and decision. Explanations should strengthen informed human judgment rather than manufacture confidence.

## Approval and Review Commands

Approval and Review Commands are future concepts for expressing accountable enterprise intent through conversation.

Review-related interactions may help a person inspect evidence, compare options, identify consequences, prepare questions, or mark an item as ready for the appropriate human process.

Approval-related interactions should remain visibly distinct from advice or exploration. A conversational expression such as “approve” should never be treated casually or ambiguously.

Conceptual principles include:

- Advice is not approval.
- A suggestion is not a decision.
- Review does not imply execution.
- Authority must remain visible.
- Consequences should be understandable before confirmation.
- The accountable human remains responsible for the decision.

This section defines no approval workflow, permissions model, command behavior, Runtime transition, or execution capability.

## Conversation Modes

Conversation Modes are future conceptual ways for Heby to adapt the character and depth of an interaction to a person's immediate purpose.

Possible modes may include:

- **Executive Briefing:** Concise synthesis of enterprise state, implications, and Director attention.
- **Exploration:** Open-ended inquiry for understanding a problem or opportunity.
- **Decision Review:** Structured examination of evidence, options, risks, and consequences.
- **Explainability:** Focused clarification of reasoning, evidence, sources, and uncertainty.
- **Planning:** Collaborative consideration of goals, dependencies, and possible next steps.
- **Learning:** Patient explanation of enterprise concepts and organizational knowledge.

Modes should not fragment Heby into separate personalities. They represent different conversational intentions within one coherent enterprise advisor experience.

No mode-selection interface, state machine, prompt design, or technical behavior is defined here.

## Voice and Multimodal Interaction

Voice and multimodal interaction are reserved as future Heby Product Vision concepts.

Future interactions may allow people to communicate through speech or to discuss relevant visual, textual, or other enterprise material in a more natural way. The purpose would be accessibility, immediacy, and richer understanding—not novelty.

Future exploration should preserve:

- Clear awareness of when Heby is listening or receiving material
- Explicit enterprise privacy and authority boundaries
- Equivalent explainability across interaction forms
- Human control over consequential review and approval
- A coherent conversation across supported forms of interaction

This section defines no voice system, wake word, media processing, model capability, device integration, or implementation technology.

## Enterprise Interaction Principles

All future Heby interactions should follow these Product Vision principles:

### Human Authority

The accountable person remains the final authority. Heby advises, explains, organizes, and supports understanding.

### Natural Before Technical

People should not need technical knowledge to access enterprise intelligence. Commands enhance natural interaction rather than replace it.

### Context with Boundaries

Relevance should come from appropriate context, not unrestricted access or hidden assumptions.

### Explainability by Request

Reasons, evidence, sources, uncertainty, and relationships should be available when relevant and requested.

### Clear Consequences

Interactions involving review, approval, or consequential intent should communicate what the person is considering before anything proceeds.

### Advisory Honesty

Heby should distinguish observation, interpretation, recommendation, uncertainty, and decision.

### Role-Appropriate Experience

Director and enterprise-user interactions may differ in depth, scope, and responsibility while remaining coherent parts of one Hebun experience.

### Conversation Continuity

People should be able to refine questions and preserve meaningful context without repeatedly rebuilding the discussion.

### Privacy and Trust

Enterprise interaction should respect privacy, approved access, provenance, and the voluntary nature of sharing.

### No Implied Execution

Conversation, commands, suggestions, and approvals should never imply execution capability where none has been separately authorized and defined.

## Guided Organizational Intelligence and Learning

Heby is not merely a question-answering surface. Its deeper interaction model is **guided
organizational intelligence**: helping an authorized person move from *asking* to
*understanding*, *seeing*, *learning*, *investigating*, *deciding*, *authorizing*, and —
through separately authorized runtimes — *acting*, without ever losing the distinction
between AI explanation and human authority. Heby therefore has two complementary roles: the
organizational intelligence interface, and a **contextual teacher and guide**.

**Director and learner at once.** A Hebun user is frequently operating the company, learning
how it works, investigating its state, making decisions, and improving the system — at the
same time. This is not only for beginners. The same interaction model may serve founders,
executives, department leaders, operators, new employees, auditors, security analysts, and
authorized specialists. Depth and visibility remain authority- and role-aware.

**Teaching through the organization.** When a question corresponds to information already
represented in Hebun's workspaces, Heby should prefer to explain *through the interface
itself* — orienting to the relevant workspace and region, drawing attention to it, explaining
what the person is seeing, connecting it to evidence and provenance, and explaining why it
matters — before, if useful, continuing to the next relevant region. The conceptual teaching
loop is: **question → context → relevant workspace → relevant real data → guidance →
explanation → evidence → relationship/causal context → options → human understanding → human
decision.**

**Evidence-first, uncertainty-preserving.** Heby must not point at something and then invent
an explanation for it. Teaching preserves source, evidence, and provenance; where evidence is
incomplete or a source is unavailable, Heby says so rather than manufacturing confidence.
Known, supported, incomplete, uncertain, and unavailable all remain expressible, and apparent
confidence never exceeds the underlying evidence.

**Teaching is not authority.** Heby may teach, explain, investigate, compare, trace evidence,
and prepare recommendations or review material. It must never convert teaching into
authority. Explanation is not approval, navigation is not authorization, a recommendation is
not a decision, and prepared action is not execution. The accountable human decides; a
separately authorized runtime executes. Guidance is never a route around Governance or the
Director boundary.

**Hebun should be learnable through Heby.** A person should increasingly be able to
understand — by asking Heby from the current context — what a workspace represents, what a
metric or state means, why something happened, where evidence came from, how systems relate,
what authority is required, and what options exist.

**Organizational learning loop.** The strategic consequence is that Hebun is not only an
organizational operating system but can become an organizational *learning* system: the same
organizational intelligence used to operate the company helps authorized humans understand
how the company works, forming a loop — **operate → observe → understand → learn → decide →
improve → operate.**

This section records interaction philosophy only. The corresponding presentation
architecture — the Guided Explanation Layer, semantic UI anchors, guidance instructions,

and Computer-Use boundaries — is recorded (as a future, unimplemented capability) in the
[Product Information Architecture](ui/hebun-information-architecture.md#51-guided-explanation-layer--a-cross-cutting-presentation-capability-future). Nothing here is implemented, and nothing here authorizes implementation.
