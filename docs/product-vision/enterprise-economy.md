# Enterprise Economy

## Document Status

**Document type:** Living Product Vision  
**Capability status:** FUTURE PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE CAPABILITY — NOT OPEN

This document records long-term product ideas for Hebun AI. It does not authorize implementation, define architecture or Runtime behavior, establish constitutional ownership, assign Program or Phase numbers, or change the Hebun roadmap.

The Enterprise Economy is entirely optional. Every enterprise is private by default. Publishing and marketplace participation are always opt-in and require explicit Director approval.

## Vision

Enterprise Economy is a future commercial ecosystem for transforming enterprise intelligence into reusable, licensable, shareable, and revenue-generating operating assets.

Hebun may become the platform where companies publish, exchange, adapt, and monetize enterprise operating knowledge. The ecosystem would allow organizations to benefit from proven experience while retaining control over their own knowledge, identity, and operating context.

The intended product experience is not a conventional software marketplace. It is an economy for enterprise experience.

## Core Principle

> Hebun does not sell software. Hebun enables companies to exchange enterprise intelligence.

The marketplace distributes experience rather than code. Its value comes from reusable operating knowledge: how enterprises organize, decide, govern, learn, coordinate, and improve.

Marketplace assets are starting points for informed adaptation. They are never a substitute for enterprise judgment, compatibility analysis, simulation, approval, or observation.

## Enterprise Domain Framework (EDF)

**Status: FUTURE PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE FRAMEWORK — NOT OPEN**

The Enterprise Domain Framework is a future product standard that defines the universal structure every Enterprise Domain should follow. Regardless of industry or business function, a Domain should expose a predictable structure and user experience while expressing the specialist knowledge of its field.

This framework is a product vision only. It defines no architecture, Runtime, API, database, implementation, constitutional ownership, delivery lifecycle, or authority.

### EDF Core Principle

Enterprise Domains differ in business purpose. They do not differ in structural design.

Every Domain follows one common framework. A Domain specializes the framework for its particular business context; it does not invent a competing structural model.

### Universal Domain Structure

Every Enterprise Domain is conceptually composed of:

- **Domain Identity:** The Domain's purpose, scope, vocabulary, boundaries, and enterprise context.
- **People:** The people who participate in, influence, or are affected by the Domain.
- **Roles:** Recognizable functional roles within the Domain.
- **Responsibilities:** Accountabilities and expected contributions associated with those roles.
- **Organizational Structure:** The Domain's relationship to teams, functions, leadership, and the wider enterprise.
- **Processes:** Repeatable operating patterns through which the Domain creates value.
- **Workflows:** Coordinated sequences of work, review, handoff, and completion.
- **AI Agents:** Specialized advisory or bounded capability concepts relevant to the Domain.
- **Knowledge:** The contextual intelligence required to understand and operate the Domain.
- **Documents:** The records, evidence, references, and artifacts used by the Domain.
- **Decisions:** The judgments, choices, and accountable outcomes associated with the Domain.
- **Timeline:** The chronological record of meaningful Domain events.
- **Enterprise Memory:** The durable context that preserves why the Domain changed and what it learned.
- **KPIs:** Indicators that communicate performance, outcomes, risk, and progress.
- **Dashboards:** Domain-specific views that make operating context understandable.
- **Policies:** The rules and expectations relevant to Domain behavior.
- **Approvals:** The explicit review and authorization points required by the enterprise.
- **Reports:** Structured summaries for observation, accountability, and learning.
- **Integrations:** Conceptual connections between the Domain and the enterprise's surrounding systems and capabilities.
- **Marketplace Metadata:** Product information supporting discovery, compatibility, provenance, licensing, version awareness, and support expectations.

Individual Domains specialize these components through their own terminology, knowledge, operating practices, and user experience. They never replace the common framework.

### Relationship to the Enterprise Core

Every Domain consumes the Enterprise Core. The Enterprise Core remains common across every company and supplies the shared enterprise context within which Domains operate.

Conceptual Enterprise Core capabilities include:

- Director

- Decision Intelligence
- Memory
- Governance

No Domain replaces or duplicates the Enterprise Core. A Domain contributes specialized enterprise context while relying on the Core for the common product experience.

This relationship is recorded as product vision only and does not define architectural dependencies, ownership, interfaces, or Runtime behavior.

### Domain Independence

Enterprise Domains remain modular. A company may conceptually:

- Install a Domain
- Remove a Domain
- Disable a Domain
- Upgrade a Domain
- Replace a Domain
- Extend a Domain

Independent lifecycle choices should preserve a coherent enterprise experience and respect the Domain's relationship to the Enterprise Core. This principle does not specify implementation or technical modularity.

### Marketplace Compatibility

Alignment means that assets express their contribution through recognizable Domain components and clearly communicate scope, compatibility, provenance, and adaptation needs. EDF compliance would not by itself imply verification, approval, quality, or deployment authority.

Before recommending installation, Heby may evaluate:

Heby's evaluation remains advisory. It may explain compatibility, gaps, dependencies, and adaptation needs, but it does not approve installation or authorize action.

The future product lifecycle of an Enterprise Domain is conceptually:

This lifecycle is conceptual only. It defines no Runtime states, transitions, workflows, storage, automation, or implementation behavior.

## Enterprise Core Evolution

**Status: FUTURE PRODUCT EVOLUTION FRAMEWORK — NOT OPEN**

### Evolution Vision

The Enterprise Core is designed for long-term stability. It is also intentionally designed to evolve over many years rather than become permanently frozen.

Core evolution should be deliberate, rare, reviewed, and backward compatible. Evolution should protect the shared enterprise experience while allowing capabilities of lasting universal value to become part of that experience over time.

This framework is a Product Vision concept only. It defines no architecture, Runtime, API, database, implementation, constitutional authority, governance mechanism, Program, Phase, or delivery commitment. It authorizes nothing.

### Core Philosophy

The Enterprise Core is:

- **Stable:** Enterprises should be able to depend on its enduring concepts.
- **Predictable:** Evolution should be understandable, intentional, and explainable.
- **Backward Compatible:** New Core concepts should preserve existing enterprise investments and experiences.
- **Technology Independent:** Core meaning should not depend on a particular technical generation.
- **Vendor Independent:** Core concepts should remain independent of any external provider or vendor.
- **Implementation Independent:** Product meaning should endure across implementation choices.
- **Expandable:** The Core should be capable of accepting universally foundational concepts when justified.
- **Never Frozen:** Long-term stability should not prevent deliberate evolution.
- **Never Randomly Extended:** Novelty, availability, or short-term demand alone should never justify Core inclusion.

Stability and evolution are complementary principles. Stability protects the enterprise; disciplined evolution protects the Core's long-term relevance.

### Core Promotion

**Core Promotion** is the conceptual process through which a capability may become a candidate for inclusion in the Enterprise Core.

A capability may initially exist as:

- An optional Product capability
- A Marketplace asset
- An Enterprise Domain capability

After long-term use and validation, it may become a candidate for Enterprise Core inclusion. Promotion is never automatic. Adoption, popularity, commercial success, or technical availability does not independently grant Core status.

Every promotion requires explicit Director approval. This product-vision principle does not define the constitutional process, architectural ownership, or implementation mechanism for such approval.

### Promotion Criteria

A future promotion candidate may be evaluated conceptually against criteria such as:

- **Enterprise-wide usefulness:** It creates durable value across the enterprise rather than within one narrow context.
- **Cross-domain applicability:** It is useful across multiple Enterprise Domains.
- **Architectural maturity:** Its product meaning has become coherent, proven, and stable enough for long-term use.
- **Long-term stability:** It is unlikely to depend on a temporary trend or short-lived product pattern.
- **Backward compatibility:** Its inclusion can preserve existing enterprise experiences and investments.
- **Adoption across enterprises:** It has demonstrated value in varied organizational contexts.
- **Strategic importance:** It has become foundational to Hebun's enduring enterprise purpose.
- **Low implementation coupling:** Its conceptual value does not depend on one technology, vendor, or implementation.
- **High conceptual value:** It strengthens the common enterprise model rather than merely adding functionality.

These are product-vision considerations, not a scoring system, approval rule, or constitutional test.

### Core Protection

The Enterprise Core is protected from unnecessary growth.

Every new capability should begin outside the Core whenever possible. This creates space for experimentation, adaptation, specialization, and evidence without destabilizing the shared enterprise foundation.

Only capabilities that become universally foundational should be considered for promotion. A capability should remain optional when its value is specialized, contextual, immature, technology-dependent, or better expressed through a Domain or Marketplace asset.

Core Promotion should reduce fragmentation and strengthen shared meaning. It should never become a shortcut for increasing product visibility or permanence.

### Conceptual Capability Lifecycle

The future product lifecycle of a possible Core capability is conceptually:

1. **Idea** — A possible enterprise capability is identified.
2. **Experiment** — Its value and product meaning are explored outside the Core.
3. **Product Capability** — It becomes an optional, recognizable Hebun product capability.
4. **Marketplace Adoption** — It may be shared, adapted, and evaluated across participating enterprises.
5. **Enterprise Validation** — Long-term usefulness, stability, compatibility, and cross-domain value become observable.
6. **Promotion Candidate** — The capability is recognized as potentially universal and foundational.
7. **Director Review** — The candidate receives explicit accountable review.
8. **Enterprise Core** — Only after approval may it conceptually become part of the common Core.

This lifecycle is conceptual only. It defines no Runtime states, automated transitions, governance procedure, implementation sequence, Program, or Phase.

### Core Compatibility

Future Core evolution must preserve:

- Existing Enterprises
- Existing Domains
- Existing Marketplace assets
- Existing Product capabilities
- Existing organizational knowledge

No promotion should invalidate previous enterprise investments. Core evolution should allow established Domains, assets, capabilities, and knowledge to continue functioning conceptually without forced reinvention or loss of meaning.

Backward compatibility includes more than technical compatibility. It also protects terminology, decisions, operating knowledge, organizational continuity, and the trust enterprises place in the Hebun product model.

## Enterprise Neural Interface (ENI)

**Status: FUTURE PRODUCT VISION — NOT OPEN**

### ENI Vision

Enterprise Neural Interface is the visual representation of Enterprise Intelligence.

It may allow the Director to observe how Heby coordinates enterprise reasoning across Enterprise Domains. Its purpose is not decoration. Its purpose is transparency, awareness, trust, and enterprise understanding.

ENI is envisioned as an executive visualization layer rather than a standard enterprise interface. It represents the relationships and reasoning context through which the enterprise becomes understandable to its Director.

This vision authorizes nothing. It defines no product architecture, implementation, Runtime behavior, or delivery commitment.

### Director-Exclusive Experience

Enterprise Neural Interface is reserved exclusively for the Enterprise Director experience.

It is not visible to standard enterprise users. It is not part of normal employee interfaces or the standard enterprise experience.

Future enterprise editions may introduce limited variants for other contexts. The complete Enterprise Neural Interface experience remains Director-exclusive.

Director exclusivity expresses the intended product experience only. It does not define authentication, authorization, identity architecture, permissions, or implementation behavior.

### Enterprise Intelligence Core

Within the ENI concept, Heby appears as the **Enterprise Intelligence Core**.

Enterprise Domains appear as connected intelligence nodes surrounding or relating to that Core. Conceptual nodes may include:

- Finance
- Operations
- Knowledge
- People
- Sales
- Organization
- Security
- Timeline
- Decisions
- Agents

Additional Domains may appear over time as the enterprise evolves. A visible node represents a meaningful participant in enterprise reasoning; it does not represent a technical service, process, database, network, or infrastructure component.

### Intelligence Flow

When Heby performs enterprise reasoning, only the participating Domains should become visually active.

Enterprise intelligence should appear as living information flowing among relevant Domains and the Enterprise Intelligence Core. The visualization represents reasoning paths rather than data movement.

An active connection may conceptually communicate that a Domain contributes context, evidence, constraints, knowledge, or decision relevance. Inactive Domains should remain calm rather than create unnecessary visual activity.

The interface should help the Director understand which parts of the enterprise informed a recommendation or conclusion without implying that the visualization exposes literal internal computation.

### Design Principles

The Enterprise Neural Interface should be:

- **Calm:** It supports executive attention rather than competing for it.
- **Elegant:** Its visual language remains refined and understandable.
- **Minimal:** It reveals only information that contributes meaning.
- **Living:** It conveys that enterprise intelligence is active and contextual.
- **Organic:** Relationships should feel connected rather than mechanically diagrammed.
- **Never distracting:** Motion or change should not interrupt Director judgment.
- **Never decorative:** Every visible state should communicate relevant enterprise meaning.
- **Always meaningful:** Activity, emphasis, and relationships should remain explainable.

Visual restraint is foundational. More activity does not imply more intelligence.

### Director Benefits

The conceptual goals of ENI are to help the Director:

- Understand enterprise reasoning
- Observe Domain participation
- Increase confidence in enterprise intelligence
- Improve explainability
- Reveal enterprise relationships
- Strengthen Director awareness

ENI should make complex enterprise participation easier to comprehend without replacing evidence, accountable review, or Director judgment.

### Product Vision Constraints

Enterprise Neural Interface defines:

- No Runtime
- No rendering engine
- No animation technology
- No APIs
- No databases
- No AI implementation
- No Computer Use
- No infrastructure
- No execution

The visualization represents enterprise reasoning only. It does not authorize actions, move enterprise data, control systems, expose model internals, or grant Heby execution authority.

## Installation Experience

The future installation experience is envisioned as a governed adaptation journey:

1. **Install** — Select an asset as a candidate for evaluation.
2. **Analyze Enterprise** — Understand the receiving enterprise's operating context.
3. **Compatibility Analysis** — Evaluate fit, dependencies, conflicts, and readiness.
4. **Adaptation Plan** — Define how the asset should change for local needs.
5. **Simulation** — Explore expected behavior and consequences before adoption.
6. **Approval** — Obtain explicit accountable approval.
7. **Deployment** — Introduce the approved adaptation through a controlled process.
8. **Observation** — Monitor outcomes, fit, risks, and unintended effects.
9. **Optimization** — Improve the adaptation using observed evidence.

Blueprints are adapted, never blindly copied. “Install” describes the beginning of evaluation, not automatic deployment or authority.
